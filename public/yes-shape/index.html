Mechanical Engineering Lead · Complex Electromechanical Systems · Prototype to Production

The seat

Hands-on mechanical engineering leadership. Complex electromechanical systems taken from concept through prototyping, validation, and production ramp: robotics platforms, precision mechanisms, sealed consumer and industrial hardware. The mandate that gets my best work: run the program, build the team, own the architecture and the integration, and leave the process better than I found it.

How that looks in practice: I ask each engineer what they want to own. The picks are usually sensible. We divide the work, I take what remains, plus integration, plus the parts nobody wants until they see them working. People who want the work get room and backup.

Hard filters

Bay Area onsite or remote. I am local to the Peninsula. Relocation is off the table, everywhere, permanently.
Non-weaponized work only. I build machines that keep people out of harm's way. I do not build weapon systems. This filter is not negotiable and no compensation number moves it.
Real mandate, in writing. Accountability and authority arrive together or not at all: team, budget, architecture, and honest schedules.

Evidence

Cobotic food-robotics makeline 70+ integrated actuators, safety architecture, pilot deployment through production. Mechanism reliability at scale Movement architecture proven across more than 3,000,000 logged cycles. Wearable mechanisms and NPI Lifecycle validation, overseas factory ramp, yield recovery. System architecture Multidisciplinary integration and technical leadership, concept to manufacturing.

What to send

  1. The role and its actual reporting line.
  2. The mandate: what is stuck, what must change, and by when.
  3. Team state: current headcount versus build-out target.
  4. The compensation band, cash versus equity.
  5. The location model, stated plainly.

I answer fast, including fast nos. A precise no now beats a slow maybe, for both of us.
