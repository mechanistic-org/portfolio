The operator's manual

Machines ship with manuals. People mostly don't. This is the index for working with me: the instruments, the interfaces, and the operating norms, human-readable and machine-readable.

The instruments

The yes-shape For recruiters and hiring teams: the seat I take, the lines I hold, exactly what to send. Opportunity diligence How I decide: evidence tiers, primary-source passes, models, gates, decision records. How I work The method pillar: operational capability paired directly with project evidence. About Who I am and where the judgment comes from. Resume The record, in standard form. Projects The evidence: shipped hardware, forensics, and the work itself.

Operating norms

Distilled from the working record, not aspirations. These are the load-bearing rules that already govern how I run.

Direct, and evidence-governed.

I say what the evidence says, and I yield to evidence, not to pressure. If a question is framed wrong, I will say so before answering inside the frame.

Decisions get sized before they get ceremony.

Reversible calls get made and noted, not queued for permission. Destructive, outward-facing, or hard-to-reverse calls get asked about first. Every ruling that matters gets written down with its reasons.

Uncertainty gets flagged, never papered.

Extrapolation is labeled as extrapolation. Gaps do not get filled with confident-sounding inference. Claimed and modeled numbers never get repeated as fact.

Fix the process, not the symptom.

When a defined process throws an error, the process gets stopped and repaired, not worked around. An alarm from an instrument is a signal about a misconfiguration, never a thing to silence for a green board.

Feedback lands when it carries the why.

Bring the observation and the reasoning behind it, and I will take it straight, no softening required. I am genuinely willing to be wrong; it just takes evidence rather than friction.

Working conditions and hours

Normal working hours are the default. I prefer remote work and a quiet place to concentrate; open-plan offices do not suit me.

During builds and bring-up, I'll be in the office, lab, or shop with the hardware. Once we're through that phase, I prefer to return to focused remote work. Travel is selective; extended onsite stays are no longer my default way to solve a problem.

I can get deeply absorbed in build or discovery work and continue into evenings or weekends.

What I need from a team

Give me a clear mandate, the resources to carry it out, and authority that matches the accountability. I ask engineers what they want to own, divide the work, and take responsibility for integration. People get room and backup.

How I learn

I learn by working through the actual problem and understanding why something behaves as it does. If I don't understand, I'll say so. A better explanation or a quicker route to the truth gets my attention.

Focus and interruptions

When I'm locked in, switching context is difficult. Ping first, include the context, and say what needs a decision. If it's an emergency, call. New evidence or a clear reason to change direction helps me switch.

Feedback and disagreement

Be direct. Bring the observation and the reasoning behind it. I want commitments and disagreements made explicit, with a path toward resolving them. If you show me a better answer, I'll change direction and put my effort into it. The best idea wins.
