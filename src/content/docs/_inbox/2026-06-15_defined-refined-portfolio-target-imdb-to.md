The refined target for the portfolio's presentation was established, combining an IMDb-style top section with a 3-column body (HUD on left, article in center, media/references on right). This design incorporates light/dark/system theming, an interactive cast widget, and various media primitives, all built modularly via a Wikipedia-template-modeled primitive library to support over 100 pages. This pivot was made after judging the Hyperspace scrolly design 'flat and incoherent,' moving to a Wikipedia-Manual of Style (MoS) base, demoting scrolly to an optional accent. The infinite canvas concept was clarified to function as an 'atlas' or index/explore layer, deep-linking into flagship pages. A 'holy-grail' 3-column layout was defined with a sticky section Table of Contents, main body, and an infobox for key facts/metrics. The infobox was established as the definitive solution for the HUD struggle, providing a conventional, always-visible, and scannable panel for key facts and forensic metrics. Legibility and scale are prioritized over a bespoke 'cyberspace' look, with distinctiveness from content density. `ProjectArticle` was promoted as the default base layout, with Hyperspace demoted to opt-in. Wikipedia's template taxonomy was found adoptable for Astro, providing a structural solution for modularity and scalability for 100+ projects via a 'component index' or primitive library. The philosophy for images inverted Wikipedia's 'minimize images' default, making forensic photos first-class while retaining presentation discipline, with galleries presented as real captioned figures. A `MediaHeader` primitive was defined for the top of project pages, drawing from IMDb. The open design question of HUD/metrics distribution and interactivity was resolved with a hybrid approach of collapsible widget cards and tabs, placing scars in the left HUD rail. A Cast widget with LinkedIn hover functionality was developed for the primitive library. The `ProjectArticle` spike successfully rendered c24 at `/spike/c24`, validating the Wikipedia-MoS base and proving the decoupling architecture.
