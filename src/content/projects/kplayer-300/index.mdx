## Designing the complete player

I designed and engineered Kaleidescape’s KPLAYER-300, known internally as Mercury. The compact player had to work in a rack shelf and behind a display, which we expected to be one of its most common installations. {/* ^[evidence:EV-52c017a6e377] ^[evidence:EV-342c421dc439] */}

My work spanned the enclosure surfacing and blinded vent pattern, the EMI liners and Acheson coating on the plastics, the bezel glow board, and the main PCB layout and placement. I also designed the custom feet, fans, cooling system and fan grommets. {/* ^[evidence:EV-342c421dc439] */}

## Openings without exposing the interior

The enclosure used a blinded, labyrinth vent pattern. It gave the box ventilation openings while concealing the interior from view. The venting was part of the product’s appearance as well as its cooling layout. {/* ^[evidence:EV-52c017a6e377] ^[evidence:EV-342c421dc439] */}

## A continuous shield inside a plastic enclosure

The metallic liners and the coating worked together. The Acheson coating on the plastic parts tied the liners together into an integral Faraday-cage shield. {/* ^[evidence:EV-52c017a6e377] */}

## Tuning the blue glow and isolating the fans

I tuned the glow board’s LED spacing and offset to meet Kaleidescape’s strict blue-glow visual identity. The fan mounts addressed a different aspect of the product experience: elastomeric grommets isolated the fans, reducing noise and improving noise, vibration and harshness. {/* ^[evidence:EV-52c017a6e377] ^[evidence:EV-342c421dc439] */}

## Power supplies and the rear-shield interface

Initial power-supply problems forced us to evaluate several types, with different bodies, connections and cable routes to accommodate in the shelf. The CAD layouts below show some of those packaging alternatives. {/* ^[evidence:EV-342c421dc439] */}

During EMI testing in June 2008, Greg Curry and Adrian reported a LAN-conducted emissions pass after isolating the Ethernet connector from the metal rear plate. Gabe Cohn supplied revised shield CAD, and I compared the old and new geometry as the clearance requirement moved into the mechanical design. The reported pass applied to the configuration under test. {/* ^[evidence:EV-adcd0de2686d] */}

## Testing the required installation

The shelf configuration was part of the product requirement. In August 2008, Nathan Forster reported a Mercury thermal pass in a crowded rack, then reported no component-temperature difference between the shelf venting arrangements he tested. That result supported considering a simpler shelf; it did not make the player’s own case vents unnecessary. {/* ^[evidence:EV-342c421dc439] ^[evidence:EV-adcd0de2686d] */}

The installation drawings also had to communicate the stack. After a dealer interpreted the height as accumulating with each shelf, Linus Wong asked for a stacked view and I supplied a revised drawing candidate. The review addressed clearance above the top shelf as well as individual dimensions. {/* ^[evidence:EV-f5d9a739ea7e] */}

## From KPLAYER-300 to M300

KPLAYER-300 quickly evolved into the M300 during production. The physical products were virtually identical; the processor architecture changed to support Blu-ray playback. The mechanical design carried forward as the media platform evolved. {/* ^[evidence:EV-342c421dc439] */}
