I. PROJECT SUMMARY
• Role: Base Station (Wall Plate/Chassis)
• Mandate: Provide structural mounting, power interface, and mechanical retention for Sativa/Elvis control modules while managing high-voltage termination.
• Core Achievement: Scheduled 1,500 unit production run for the July 2017 ramp, despite critical mechanical seizures during validation.
II. THE ANATOMY OF FAILURE (Heuristic Analysis)
Discovery Heuristics Applied: Tolerance Stackup, Thermal Deferral, and Yield Crisis.
• The Trigger (Crisis):
◦ Total Seizure: The mechanism failed 100% of dust ingress removal tests. 14 out of 14 units seized in the chassis, creating an operational deadlock where the DUT (Device Under Test) could not be removed.
◦ Structural Deformation: "Large square dimples" used for spacing the slide-lock hooks caused stress concentrations, deforming the immediate surrounding areas and compromising the fit.
◦ Thermal Deferral: We were forced to defer worst-case thermal testing (45.3℃) to EVT2 because the v0.005 software could not drive the LCM (Liquid Crystal Module) to the necessary conditions.
• The Intervention (Fix):
◦ Geometry Modification: I proposed replacing the square dimples with smaller circular dimples of the same depth to distribute stress and mitigate warping.
◦ Scope Containment: I formally rejected the 10-year lifecycle requirement during EVT2. This was a retroactive mandate not present in the initial architecture, and the mechanical structure was not designed to survive it.
◦ Vendor Escalation: Directed Locoroll to demand the vendor (Bel) resolve the critical EMI noise source isolated to the Bazooka architecture during EVT2.

◦ Yield: Logged 5 distinct mechanical defects in the EVT2 chassis design, including warping, burrs, and inconsistent hook tension.

What are the core design requirements for the Bazooka product?

Forensic Audit: Bazooka Product Requirements
CONTEXT: The following design requirements are extracted from the Product Requirements Document (PRD) hierarchy, specifically contrasting the initial 2016 baseline (040-00014_rev02.pdf) against the 2017 revision (Bazooka-V1-03-May-2017).
MANDATE: Bazooka serves as the Base Station (Wall Plate/Chassis), providing structural mounting, high-voltage termination, and a power interface for the Sativa (Master) and Elvis (Extension) control modules.
I. ARCHITECTURAL PRIORITIES
The documented constraint hierarchy for the Bazooka architecture is explicitly ranked:
1. Feature (Functionality)
2. Size (Fitment)
3. Cost (BOM targets)
II. ELECTRICAL SPECIFICATIONS (High Voltage & Harvesting)
The electrical architecture is designed to bridge high-voltage mains with low-voltage logic, featuring a critical "Energy Harvesting" requirement for installations lacking a neutral wire.
• Input Power: 100 to 132VAC at 50/60Hz (Single Phase).
• Load Capacity:
◦ 2016 Baseline: 650W electronic switch with phase angle control.
◦ 2017 Revision: Increased to 850W electronic switch.
• Power Interface (Auxiliary):
◦ Must provide isolated output to the mating controller (Sativa/Elvis).
◦ Voltage: 5.5V (Typ) or 4.95V (Typ).
◦ Current: 1A maximum, with reduced output during "energy harvesting" mode (no neutral wire present).
• Quiescent Power: Must consume \<0.6W (2016) or \<1.1W (2017) when idle.
• Surge Protection: Rated for 2.5KV.
III. MECHANICAL CONSTRAINTS (The Chassis)
The mechanical design is governed by the need to fit into standard residential infrastructure while managing the physical "Air Gap" safety feature.
• Enclosure Dimensions: 105 x 44 x 32 mm.
• Fitment: Base and wires must fit within 95% of residential light switch electrical boxes (3” x 2” x 1⅞“).
• Air Gap Switch: A mechanical switch is required to physically disengage the AC line connection when the mating assembly is removed.
◦ Cycle Life: Rated for 10,000 cycles.
◦ Actuation: Controlled via an external "Test" button.
• Material Composition: Aluminum front housing with a plastic rear enclosure.
IV. INTERFACE & CONTROL LOGIC
• Communication: UART interface (9600 bits/sec, 8 data bits, no parity) for status exchange with the attached controller.
• Dimming Control: Phase angle control capable of 0.01-degree precision, supporting both leading and trailing (reverse) edge dimming.
• Zero Crossing: Hardware detection required to sync phase control.
V. RELIABILITY & COMPLIANCE (The Conflict Zone)
• Regulatory: Must meet UL 1472, ICES-003, and FCC Part 15 Subpart B standards.
• Environmental: RoHS II compliant and REACH \<0.1% SVHC.
• Lifecycle Dispute:
◦ The PRD explicitly requires an Operational Lifetime of minimum 10 years.
◦ Forensic Note: This requirement was formally rejected by the engineering architect during EVT2 due to the lack of an initial mandate to support such duration in the mechanical architecture.
How does Locoroll validate product durability through reliability testing?

Forensic Audit: Durability Validation Protocol
CONTEXT: Locoroll verifies product durability through a rigorous "Waterfall Strategy", a testing hierarchy designed to sequentially stress units from environmental conditioning through to catastrophic mechanical failure. This approach allows for maximum data extraction from limited sample sizes by prioritizing "highest risk failure modes" before destructive testing.
I. THE STRATEGIC ARCHITECTURE (The 10-Year Mandate)
Validation is mathematically derived from a 10-Year Operational Lifetime requirement. Engineering calculates cycle counts based on a usage profile of 365 days/year with daily interaction, applying a 1.5x Margin Factor to ensure robustness.
• Main Switch: Validated to 54,750 cycles (10 years x 365 days x 1.5 margin x 10 cycles/day).
• Dimmer Key: Validated to 27,375 cycles.
• Extreme Force: Validated to 2,737.5 cycles (simulating pressure seen every other day).
II. MECHANICAL STRESS VALIDATION (Abuse Simulation)
Locoroll simulates specific "User Abuse" scenarios to identify structural weaknesses:
• Rapid Pull Test: Simulates a user's clothing or purse catching on the unit and ripping it from the wall. A weight is dropped via a pulley system at 45-degree angles to shock-load the mounting interface,.
• Extreme Force Test: A 100N (10kg) load is applied via a 16mm steel rod to 9 specific locations on the device face to test structural rigidity against heavy user contact,.
• Crush Test: A 30kg load is applied to the unit to simulate accidental crushing, requiring the device to maintain structural integrity.
• Rock Tumble Testing: Units are placed in a rotating drum filled with granite gravel, sand, and river pebbles for up to 60 minutes. This benchmark test simulates long-term abrasion and scratch resistance against competitor products,.
III. ENVIRONMENTAL HARDENING
Units undergo environmental pre-conditioning before mechanical stress to expose latent defects:
• Thermal Shock: Rapid temperature cycling between -20°C and 65°C (5 cycles) to stress material expansion coefficients.
• Salt Mist: Exposure to salt fog to validate the corrosion resistance of plating and metal contacts, specifically looking for oxidation on "Class I" visible surfaces,.
• Liquid Ingress (The "Coke" Test):
◦ Spill Test: 4 oz of liquid (Coke) is poured directly over the top center of the unit to simulate a beverage spill on a wall switch,.
◦ Wet Hands: Synthetic sweat solution is applied via pipette to critical touchpoints to test for chemical corrosion and ingress over 10 cycles,.
IV. YIELD & COSMETIC STANDARDS
• Drop Testing: Units are dropped from 1.3 meters onto hardwood and ceramic tile, testing 6 sides and 4 corners to ensure no safety issues or cracking of the glass (CG),.
• Surface Durability: Pencil hardness, cross-cut tape adhesion, and chemical resistance tests (e.g., Petroleum ether, artificial sweat) ensure the aesthetic finish survives the 10-year lifecycle without peeling or fading,,.
What cosmetic standards and defect tolerances govern the manufacturing process?

Forensic Audit: Manufacturing Cosmetic Standards
CONTEXT: The aesthetic integrity of the Bazooka and associated modules (Sativa/Elvis) is governed by Locoroll Specification 040-00020 ("General Cosmetic Specification"). This "Brutalist" standard establishes a zero-defect mentality for critical surfaces while defining rigid acceptance windows for secondary zones to maintain yield.
I. SURFACE ARCHITECTURE (The Classification Matrix)
Surfaces are tiered by visibility and customer interaction frequency. This hierarchy dictates the severity of inspection.
• Class A+ (Critical): Logos, lenses, and surfaces within 5mm of a logo. These require the highest level of workmanship and are constantly in customer view.
• Class A (Primary): Top or front surfaces constantly visible to the user.
• Class B (Secondary): Side and lower edges, or surfaces viewed only by rotating the assembly.
• Class C (Concealed): Bottom surfaces, backs, or internal areas only visible during disassembly.
II. THE INSPECTION GAUNTLET (Viewing Conditions)
Inspection is not subjective; it is a calibrated process defined by the "Time and Distance" method.
• Lighting: Cool white fluorescent illumination (Daylight mode), typically 850 ~ 1050 Lux.
• Distance: The standard viewing distance is 30cm (approx. 12 inches) for general inspection.
• Time:
◦ High Gloss (A1-A3): 10 seconds per surface.
◦ Non-High Gloss: 5 seconds per surface.
• Manipulation: The part is rotated vertically and horizontally (+/- 60°) to catch light reflections on defects.
III. DEFECT TOLERANCE VECTORS (Acceptance Criteria)
The specification enforces strict limits on molding and finishing anomalies. "Undefined features" like sinks, flash, or voids are generally rejected unless explicitly toleranced.
1. Dimensional Defects
• Flash: Maximum 0.05mm allowed.
• Gate Vestige: Must be flush to 0.254mm (or 0.05mm in some contexts) below the surface.
• Sharp Edges: None permitted; typically broken to a max radius of 0.5mm.
2. Visual Defects (Class A)
• Dot Defects:
◦ < 0.1mm: Ignored (considered invisible).
◦ > 0.3mm: Rejection (0 allowed).
◦ Density: Must be spaced 10mm (min) apart.
• Scratches:
◦ Class A: Max width 0.05mm, total length limited (e.g., \<12mm depending on specific criteria tables).
◦ Class B: Scratches not felt by a fingernail may be accepted using a 25% contrast rule.
• Molding Issues: Sinks, weld lines, flow marks, and gas traps are not accepted on appearance surfaces.
IV. SURFACE FINISH VALIDATION
The cosmetic durability is stress-tested to ensure the finish survives the "10-Year" mandate.
• Pencil Hardness: The surface must resist scratching from calibrated pencils.
◦ Lens: 2H hardness required.
◦ UV Coating (Low Gloss): H hardness required.
◦ Failure Note: EVT2 units failed this test (Sativa/Elvis scratched at 3/3 units).
• Cross-Cut Adhesion: A lattice pattern is cut into the paint and ripped off with 3M 610 tape.
◦ Pass Criteria: 5B (0% removal) to 3B (\<15% removal) classifications are typically acceptable.
• Abrasion (RCA/Taber):
◦ RCA: Must withstand 50 cycles minimum.
◦ Taber: Up to 300-500 cycles depending on substrate/paint contrast.
• Chemical Resistance: Surfaces are wiped with Ethanol, Isopropanol, and "Artificial Sweat" to check for staining or peeling.
What caused all 14 units to seize in the chassis?

Forensic Analysis: The "Dust Lockout" Seizure
CONTEXT: During EVT2 validation, the Bazooka architecture suffered a catastrophic mechanical deadlock. 100% of the test units (14/14) seized, rendering the Sativa and Elvis control modules irremovable from the chassis following environmental exposure,,.
THE MECHANISM OF FAILURE: The seizure was caused by a combination of tolerance stack-up, particulate friction, and structural deformation triggered by the IP5X Dust Test (ISO 12103-1 A5 fine dust),.
• The Trigger: The introduction of fine dust particles (0-80um diameter) into the slide-lock mechanism increased friction coefficients beyond the design's removal force limits.
• The Structural Flaw: The chassis utilized "large square dimples" to space the slide-lock hooks. These sharp geometries created stress concentrations, causing "deformation of immediate surrounding areas" and warping the bottom slots,,.
• The Deadlock: This deformation compromised the interference fit. When combined with the dust ingress, the assembly effectively welded itself shut, leading to a "DUT can't easy remove" failure state for 7/7 Sativa and 7/7 Elvis units,.
THE INTERVENTION: To resolve the stress concentrations causing the warp, the engineering team proposed modifying the tooling to replace the square dimples with "smaller circular dimples" of the same depth to better distribute mechanical stress and maintain planar integrity,.
How did the 10-year lifecycle rejection impact the final product?

Forensic Impact Analysis: 10-Year Lifecycle Rejection
CONTEXT: During the EVT2 phase, "The Architect" (Erik Norris) formally rejected the Product Requirements Document (PRD) mandate for an "Operational lifetime of min 10 years". This decision was a critical governance intervention rooted in the "lack of initial architectural mandate" and the reality that the mechanical structure—specifically the chassis and plating—was not designed to survive a decade of abuse.
IMPACT VECTOR 1: SCOPE CONTAINMENT & SHIPPING SCHEDULE The rejection prevented an infinite loop of validation failures that would have stalled Mass Production (MP).
• The Block: FIH (the JDM partner) explicitly flagged that the "ME structure and durability is not designed for a 10-year life" and that reliability testing was currently based on "2 years cycle simulation".
• The Release: By striking the 10-year requirement, the team bypassed impossible DVT exit criteria, allowing the schedule to advance to a 1,500 unit production run for the July 2017 ramp.
IMPACT VECTOR 2: FIELD REALITY (The 30-Month Wall) The rejection effectively acknowledged the hardware's actual lifespan limitations, which were later validated by field failure data.
• Forensic Validation: Post-launch data from retail channels (e.g., Home Depot) indicated a specific failure pattern where units failed after approximately 28 to 30 months of use.
• Root Cause: These failures were attributed to "Time Dependent Dielectric Breakdown" (TDDB) and thermal stress in the dimming circuitry, confirming that the architecture lacked the thermal and electrical headroom for a 10-year lifecycle.
IMPACT VECTOR 3: ACCEPTANCE OF "BRITTLE" YIELD The decision forced the acceptance of cosmetic and mechanical flaws that would have been instant failures under a 10-year standard.
• Aesthetic Degradation: The "Black zinc finish" on the chassis was known to chip away after multiple installation cycles. Under a 10-year spec requiring strict corrosion resistance and cosmetic integrity, this would have required a total re-tooling of the plating process.
• Mechanical Fatigue: The chassis suffered from "warping, burrs, and inconsistent hook tension". Accepting the lower lifecycle requirement allowed these defects to be classified as "yield" issues rather than "reliability" blockers.
What were the five mechanical defects found in EVT2?

Forensic Data Extraction: EVT2 Mechanical Defects
CONTEXT: During the EVT2 validation phase, the Bazooka Chassis (the structural backbone of the Base Station) exhibited critical manufacturing and design flaws.
