## The brief and my role

I contracted to Frog on a home barbecue concept for Fissler. I developed the product and proof-of-concept 3D, built and tested prototypes, and handled experimental planning, data acquisition, controller programming, logging, analysis and reports. {/* ^[evidence:EV-2d82c5d03b62] ^[evidence:EV-d8b925fbad3b] */}

## Making the brief testable

The client wanted far-infrared cooking and a butterfly configuration that could open flat for soups and hotpot. In our development work, controlling the heating intensity while cooking meat in a practical time became a constraint. I built test rigs with different Calrod heaters to characterize meats and cooking modes, and adapted other appliances for experiments. Induction units also showed promise. {/* ^[evidence:EV-2d82c5d03b62] */}

## Driving the cage from its edge

The meat cage clamped together in two halves, with teeth around the outer edges. Supported in the cooking position, it was rotated by a drive pinion at different speeds. Driving the cage from its edge kept the drive out of the space between the food and the heat source. {/* ^[evidence:EV-2d82c5d03b62] ^[evidence:EV-d8b925fbad3b] */}

## Results and decisions

The prototypes worked well in many respects, and the surviving cooking report records encouraging results alongside practical limitations: smaller pieces could shift and bind, cooking varied across the cage, and fat spatters or spilled marinades could produce smoke. In a project centered on industrial design, the working models and test results gave us evidence to discuss. I used that evidence to help make and communicate the case for a more practical, manufacturable direction. {/* ^[evidence:EV-2d82c5d03b62] ^[evidence:EV-ece8f99b1cb9] */}

## Where my involvement ended

My involvement ended with the contract. I did not follow the program's later development closely enough to claim a production outcome. {/* ^[evidence:EV-2d82c5d03b62] */}
