        - title: "The Problem"
          subtitle: "Cosmetic Side Caps"
          body: |
            The C|24 aesthetic relied on large, injection-molded ABS side caps (756mm long).
            <br/><br/>
            <strong>The Defect:</strong> During Pilot Run 1, the caps warped significantly during cooling, pulling away from the chassis by 2-3mm.
            <br/><br/>
            <strong>The Stakes:</strong> Cosmetic gaps were "Class A" defects. Re-tooling would cost $50k and delay launch by 6 weeks. <strong>Unacceptable.</strong>

        - title: "The Fix"
          subtitle: "Gravitational Fixturing"
          body: |
            <strong>Root Cause:</strong> Uneven cooling due to part length and minimal drafting.
            <br/><br/>
            <strong>Solution (Method A):</strong> I designed a "cooling fixture" that used the part's own weight. By hanging the hot parts vertically immediately after ejection, gravity naturally straightened them as they cooled.
            <br/><br/>
            <strong>Result:</strong> Zero cost. Zero delay. Perfect flatness (0.5mm tolerance) across 15,000 units.

        - title: "Sheet Metal Rescue"
          subtitle: "The 'No-Bid' Crisis"
          body: |
            The C|24 chassis was a high-precision frame requiring hundreds of welded standoffs.
            <br/><br/>
            <strong>The Crisis:</strong> Our overseas vendor (Kwanta) "no-bid" the complex Top/Bottom panels during Pilot, threatening the schedule.
            <br/><br/>
            <strong>My Solution:</strong> I executed a rapid dual-source strategy:
            <ul class="list-disc pl-4 mt-2 space-y-1 text-sm">
              <li><strong>Emergency Fabrication:</strong> Engaged Mass Precision (Local) for manual prototypes to keep the line moving.</li>
              <li><strong>The Quality War:</strong> Rejected early lots with "ripples" on bend lines and enforced a tooling change to maintain the "Pro" aesthetic.</li>
            </ul>

        - title: "The 'Gill'"
          subtitle: "Advanced Surfacing"
          body: |
            The Side Gill (P/N 9440-55175) was a signature ID element with complex "lumpy" curves that the vendor's CAD failed to interpret, leaving unsightly gaps.
            <br/><br/>
            <strong>My Execution:</strong> I took direct ownership of the surfacing, manually rebuilding the transitions in Pro/Engineer. I exported geometry in "bite-sized" STEP chunks to ensure the vendor could process the tooling paths without data loss, turning a modeling nightmare into a seamless assembly.

        - title: "PCB DFM"
          subtitle: "Collision Prevention"
          body: |
            With zero room for error, I served as gatekeeper for 17+ PCBs. My 3D analysis saved multiple board spins:
            <ul class="list-disc pl-4 mt-2 space-y-1 text-sm">
              <li><strong>MicPre Collision:</strong> Caught a vertical connector crash; forced a move to Right-Angle.</li>
              <li><strong>SubMix Ergonomics:</strong> Moved ribbon headers to the edge so factory workers could actually reach them.</li>
              <li><strong>Time Code Alignment:</strong> Found LED displays floating 8mm too high; forced a layout change to align them with the chassis window.</li>
