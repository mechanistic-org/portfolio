## Sole mechanical responsibility

I was Kaleidescape's sole mechanical engineer for Cinema One, a combination movie server and disc player. I developed the concept, layout, surfaces and mechanical BOM. My responsibility covered every item on that BOM through design, engineering, specification, testing, validation, part bring-up and production bring-up. {/* ^[evidence:EV-ed8287d7f11f] ^[evidence:EV-50fd241c87ed] ^[evidence:LK-8c67248d] */}

The broad silver body and curved white front gave the product a quiet exterior. Behind that front, I had to accommodate the disc opening, buttons, illuminated graphics and infrared receiver, then bring them together with the optical drive, hard drive, fan, electronics and chassis. Each visible interface depended on geometry elsewhere in the assembly. {/* ^[evidence:EV-50fd241c87ed] ^[evidence:LK-8ef62a80] */}

I prepared the materials and selected participants for the design competitions. Whipsaw became involved through another program I had engaged them on; their practical role on Cinema One was to review and validate my existing concept, layout and BOM. I remained responsible for developing and delivering the mechanical product. {/* ^[evidence:EV-ed8287d7f11f] */}

## Building the enclosure

I developed the cover as a molded plastic shell with separate metal stiffeners inside it. A top stiffener, left and right stiffeners, and a rear shield formed the cover assembly. Pressure-sensitive adhesive joined the shell and its stiffeners. The exterior surfaces and the internal metalwork had to work together as one enclosure. {/* ^[evidence:EV-50fd241c87ed] ^[evidence:LK-36bc04f0] ^[evidence:LK-8ef62a80] */}

The front was a separate assembly: a molded bezel frame carrying the decorated faceplate, button components and IR lens. I had to coordinate the parts where they met at the visible edges and where they located against the drive and electronics. Yomura supplied the molded cover, Mass Precision the metalwork, and Nissha the decorated faceplate. {/* ^[evidence:EV-50fd241c87ed] ^[evidence:LK-8ef62a80] */}

Assembly access mattered alongside the finished fit. The build brought together drive, fan, bezel, cover and chassis subassemblies. A fixture located the front assembly against the cover; during final assembly, the front bezel was positioned at 90 degrees to the chassis to connect its ribbon cable. Those steps required room to position and connect parts before closing the enclosure. {/* ^[evidence:LK-9b3723bf] */}

## Closing the tolerance loops

I brought in Function Engineering for tolerance analysis, drawing on our earlier work together. Their March 2013 review examined the loops between the bezel, cover, disc slot, sheet metal, alignment features and switches. One example compared the nominal 0.5 mm bezel-to-cover gap with a calculated root-sum-square variation of ±0.92 mm under the initial assumptions. The intended reveal required control of the whole assembly. {/* ^[evidence:EV-dbd9791da2bb] ^[evidence:LK-0f61ade9] */}

The options included increasing the gap, tightening molded-part tolerances and changing how the parts were located during assembly. A companion calculation with tighter tolerances reduced the statistical estimate, although its worst-case variation still exceeded the nominal gap. Vendor confirmation of the process tolerances remained part of the work. {/* ^[evidence:LK-0f61ade9] ^[evidence:LK-341ee05d] */}

The disc opening and cover installation exposed other constraints. The disc had unequal clearance above and below it. Alignment pins had limited lead-in, and the feet could change the relationship between parts during assembly. Function's review considered centering the opening, changing lead-ins and using assembly spacers. These were the interfaces I had to resolve in developing the mechanical design. {/* ^[evidence:EV-50fd241c87ed] ^[evidence:LK-0f61ade9] ^[evidence:LK-d54010d6] */}

## Carrying changes through the parts

My mechanical development work continued through the individual molded features. The April and May 2013 change records cover the bezel shroud, transfer of a slot rib to the front frame, button contact geometry, screw holes, and clearance for gate vestiges and ejector mismatch. Changes to the cover, frame, button tree and faceplate had to stay coordinated because they met at the same front assembly. {/* ^[evidence:EV-50fd241c87ed] ^[evidence:LK-02d7d7d5] ^[evidence:LK-b3d05959] */}

The IR lens was one small part of that refinement. I added an internal bounce rib in the June 9, 2013 drawing revision and relocated it in the August 7 revision. The detail changed within the existing lens and front assembly. {/* ^[evidence:EV-50fd241c87ed] ^[evidence:LK-c180c650] */}

The cover also needed attention after molding. A 2013 sample-feedback photograph questions whether flash removal had chamfered an edge that needed to remain sharp. Yomura's annotations describe difficult manual trimming and report a CNC-milled sample on October 25. Photographs made the edge condition and the supplier's proposed process work concrete enough to review together. {/* ^[evidence:LK-4d1bf860] */}

Prototype drawing releases began in October 2012, followed by production drawing releases and Cinema One's July 2013 introduction. Assembly-document revisions continued through the year, covering fasteners, torque instructions and EMI-gasket placement. My responsibility extended through this work of getting the mechanical product built. {/* ^[evidence:EV-dbd9791da2bb] ^[evidence:LK-36bc04f0] ^[evidence:LK-8c67248d] ^[evidence:LK-9b3723bf] */}

## Making appearance a concrete decision

In October 2014, I cut ten top covers so I could photograph the bend surface straight on. The depth of field was narrow, so keeping that surface parallel to the camera sensor mattered. I photographed a range of defects and asked colleagues to vote on numbered pass/fail examples. I wanted the factory's acceptance discussion to be grounded in the same visible conditions. {/* ^[evidence:EV-f8cf01c939d0] */}

Mark McKenzie, Nathan Forster and Rob Wudrick participated in the review. I reordered and renumbered the examples, then asked whether I could release the document to Sanmina. Mark approved the release; subsequent factory correspondence called for further inspection against the updated deviation. {/* ^[evidence:EV-181cc9638f36] ^[evidence:EV-8d88339190f6] */}

My ownership extended across the mechanical BOM, from the surfaces and smallest molded features through testing, validation and production bring-up. The assembly and inspection decisions were part of delivering that complete mechanical product. {/* ^[evidence:EV-ed8287d7f11f] */}
