# Backsplash: integrating the cabinet electronics

I owned the entire Backsplash subsystem and its component integration into Hyphen's Makeline. Each cabinet's power supplies, controllers and communications were housed in its Backsplash modules, alongside the display interface.

## Power and controls in the cabinet

That concentration of equipment made airflow and thermal management critical. The Backsplash modules contained the brains for each cabinet, so their component integration required a complex coordination effort.

The open-enclosure photographs make the packaging problem visible: control boards, terminal rows and cable routing share the cavity behind the display array. These parts had to come together within the repeated enclosure system.

## One display assembly across the modules

The Backsplash task estimate identifies six module configurations and calls for a common display assembly. The enclosure work depended on several interfaces being defined together: LED selections from electrical engineering, display pitch and three-dimensional location from industrial design, and CAD and connection information for an additional small board.

## The mounting grid was an interface

The mounting grid located the displays and their board interfaces within that repeated geometry. The task plan called for checking whether the conductive-spring interface between the displays and PCB assemblies would deflect the grid, using spring and FR4 data and samples. Stiffness was an explicit development requirement.

## Defining the optical and board geometry

The industrial-design packet specifies a number 4 brushed stainless-steel body and a dark tinted polycarbonate front. Thickness depended on the electronics package, while corner-radius and material-variant notes remained tentative. The optical intent combined a compact display with a polycarbonate diffuser and an LED grid behind its illuminated surround.

The specified Newhaven NHD-3.12-25664UCW2 is a 256 x 64 OLED module. The ID packet calls it LCD, but the named component's datasheet identifies it as OLED. This terminology correction does not establish a technology change or identify every installed module.

My LIGHT_RING drawing, dated July 21, 2021, defines the surround with a 4 mm band and 1.75 mm and 1.05 mm thickness dimensions. The ID packet's separate 10 mm diffuser-width note remains tentative. These are distinct design records, with a final optical stack still unestablished.

My DISPLAY_PCB drawing, dated August 25, records edge, cleanliness, hole-control and model-based definition requirements. The drawings document mechanical work within my broader subsystem responsibility. The DISPLAY_PCB drawing's unfilled approval fields leave its formal release unestablished.

## Sealing and access around the package

The task plan also addresses sealing between tray and chassis, a narrower front-frame RTV seal with a bezel or overlay, buttons on the end modules, rear power switches and a fastening mechanism. The rows apply to different module combinations. They connect the internal equipment package to the surrounding cabinet and its accessible surfaces.

## The hardware record

The photographs document a row of Backsplash modules in the Makeline assembly, an open internal package, a repeated rear mounting grid with ribbon connections, and a front array with illuminated surrounds. The bench photograph adds a closer view of the display-board package.

The physical record shows assembled hardware. It leaves final front construction, completed stiffness and sealing checks, quantified thermal performance and a Backsplash-specific production outcome unresolved. The task estimate records planned work rather than confirming each row's implementation, and the subsystem-ownership account does not assign every circuit or firmware to me.
