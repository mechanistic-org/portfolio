### Project Elmer / Zeus Forensic Report

#### I. PROJECT SUMMARY

- **Role:** Lead Mechanical Engineer (Consultant)
- **Objective:** Rapid design and fabrication of a short-run demonstration server ("Elmer," refined as "Zeus") for WebTV's HomeNet platform debut at WCS and CES 2001.
- **The Cost War:** Consumer electronics is a game of pennies. On Elmer, we didn't have pennies; we had tens of dollars. The challenge wasn't just "make it work," it was "make it cheap enough to build 50,000 month". We fought for every standoff, every bend, and every screw.
- **Core Achievement:** Engineered a custom sheet metal and machined aluminum chassis to graft PCI extensibility onto legacy "Mercury" hardware, delivering 5 functional units within a **9-day fabrication cycle**.

#### II. THE CAST

- **Erik Norris:** Project Leader / Lead Mechanical
- **Leslie Leland:** Product Manager (WebTV)
- **Val Kasvin:** Model Maker (Sputnik Models)
- **Shelly Evans:** NPI Manager (E-M Solutions)
- **Pete Schmidt:** Industrial Design (Peter Schmidt Design Assoc)

#### III. CRITICAL MECHANICAL INTERVENTIONS

**1. The "Dead Nuts" Thermal Crisis**

- **The Trigger:** The "Elmer" main board relied on an aluminum heat spreader bar for cooling. Risk: If sheet metal wasn't "**dead nuts flat**," the spreader would lose contact, causing thermal runaway.
- **The Conflict:** Engineering was torn between adding perforation holes for airflow vs maintaining solid metal for conduction.
- **The Intervention:** I removed perforation under the main PCB to maximize conduction mass, while increasing perforation between the PCB and PSU for convective airflow. Implemented "ThermaPad" gap fillers.

**2. The Hardware Standardization Failure**

- **The Trigger:** A logistical "screw-up on Elmer" regarding fastener compatibility. The prototype used a mix of metric (M3) and SAE (4-40), attempting to bridge the gap between Asian PCB standards and US fabrication shops.
- **The Fix:** I enforced a strict **4-40 UNC hardware standardization** across the entire "Zeus" assembly. I personally audited every BOM line item to ensure no metric threads reached the assembly line, preventing a "cross-threading" catastrophe during the crunch build.

**3. The Interface Collision**

- **The Trigger:** During "TitanDev" integration, the mounting hole in the top right corner threatened to collide with the Compact Flash card.
- **The Intervention:** Rapid DXF file exchange and interference checking to realign mounting points before "release to fab."

#### IV. LINKEDIN ARTIFACTS

- [ ] Engineered **5 custom server enclosures** (Zeus) and **12 node units** (Pluto) for high-visibility trade show launch.
- [ ] Reduced fabrication lead time to **9 days** for complex sheet metal assemblies including zinc plating.
- [ ] Managed **$10k+ rapid prototyping budget** for immediate turn-around of aluminum/plexiglass bezels.
- [ ] Resolved **3 critical interference issues** (CF Card, Ethernet, Power Header) prior to final fabrication release.
- [ ] Standardized **100% of assembly hardware** to 4-40 spec, eliminating assembly yield risk.

#### V. TECHNICAL STACK & GOVERNANCE

### VI. DETAILED ANALYSIS (Discrete Reports)

#### 1. Project Elmer & Zeus (Server Demo Units)

Lincicum served as the **Lead Mechanical Engineer** for these mission-critical trade show servers. This is cited as his "Core Achievement," where he delivered 5 functional units on a 9-day fabrication cycle.

- **Thermal Rescue ("The Dead Nuts Flat" Crisis):** The "Elmer" mainboard relied on an aluminum heat spreader that required a perfectly flat ("dead nuts") connection to the chassis—a tolerance impossibility for sheet metal. Lincicum executed a design change (ECO) to remove perforation holes under the heat spreader to maximize conduction mass while increasing perforation near the power supply to force convective airflow.
- **Hardware Standardization:** Following a "screw-up" on the initial Elmer prototypes where metric and SAE hardware were mixed, Lincicum enforced a strict **4-40 UNC hardware standardization** for the "Zeus" revision to prevent assembly line failures.
- **Architectural Pivot:** He rejected the ill-defined "Bastard Box" concept (a hybrid of Elmer and Mercury) to focus on refining the Elmer chassis into the "Zeus" unit, grafting PCI extensibility onto legacy hardware.
- **Vendor Management:** He orchestrated parallel workstreams between **E-M Solutions** (sheet metal/plating) and **Sputnik Models** (bezels/finishing) under a "War Room" cadence.

#### 2. Project Pluto / Titan (Node Units)

For the "HomeNet" demo nodes, Lincicum managed the mechanical integration of off-the-shelf hardware into custom enclosures.

- **Interference Resolution:** He identified and resolved critical collisions, specifically where the Compact Flash card insertion path hit mounting holes and where the Ethernet card mounting points were misaligned.
- **Rapid Data Exchange:** He managed the exchange of DXF files with the PCB team to realign connectors and mounting holes prior to fabrication.

#### 3. Project Galaxy

On the Galaxy project (a mass-produced set-top box), Lincicum provided high-level Pro/ENGINEER (Pro/E) design support and mentorship to the internal team.

- **Component Design:** He designed specific sub-assemblies, including the **"CPU Puck"** (Top and Bottom) and HDD mounting brackets.
- **Tooling Feasibility:** He worked with vendors like NT Vision to validate that the "spherical dimples" and EMI shielding concepts were suitable for high-volume progressive die tooling.
- **Mentorship:** He supported internal engineer Frank Salinas with Pro/E software techniques, drawing formats, and database management.

#### 4. Project Mercury LC (Low Cost)

Lincicum led the mechanical development for this cost-reduced version of the Mercury receiver.

- **Cost Analysis Modeling:** He generated parallel databases ("Crown" vs. "Flat" top) to allow management to obtain comparative quotes and determine the cost impact of aesthetic changes.
- **Drive Integration:** He designed a removable drive bracket that allowed the system depth to be reduced to 265mm.
- **Surface Modeling:** He generated preliminary surface data based on Industrial Design files from NewDealDesign.

#### 5. Project Cortez (Wireless Keyboard)

Lincicum acted as the expert Mechanical Engineer and Pro/E file creator for this reference design keyboard.

- **Database Creation:** He built the primary Pro/E database for the keyboard.
- **Industrial Design Integration:** He collaborated with Jeff Jones (PSDA) to resolve ergonomic issues identified in hard models, such as adjusting the keyboard tilt (3-15 degrees), refining "puffy" corners, and sharpening edge definitions.
- **Supplier Liaison:** He interfaced with **Silitek** to ensure the mechanical enclosure accommodated the key switch matrix, battery compartments, and IR lens locations.

#### 6. Xbox (Microsoft)

Following the WebTV integration into Microsoft's "eHome" group, Lincicum transitioned to supporting the Xbox console.

- **Chassis Modifications:** He worked on modifications to the bottom EMI liner and HDD bracket to eliminate power supply insulators.
- **Mass Property Studies:** He conducted weight analyses of the chassis components.
- **Component Engineering:** He designed "bridge-type" carriers for the DVD and HDD modules.

### VII. MANUFACTURING & TRIAGE

#### Manufacturing Strategy: Parallelism and Tooling Substitutions

To meet the hard deadline of CES 2001 (January) and the Western Cable Show (December), the team executed a "split-build" strategy:

- **Bifurcated Fabrication:** The build was split between **E-M Solutions** (handling sheet metal and plating) and **Sputnik Models** (handling machined plastic bezels and finishing). This allowed the chassis structural work to proceed while the cosmetic "skins" were being machined simultaneously.
- **Tooling Hacks:** Due to the 5-7 day turnaround requirement, the manufacturing vendor (EMS) could not create custom form tools. Instead, they substituted design features with "tools on hand" or utilized laser cutting for flat patterns where punching would normally be used, specifically noting that lasering electro-galvanized material was not recommended due to burn marks, forcing a switch to Cold Rolled Steel (CRS) with post-plating.
- **The "Bastard Box" Pivot:** The initial "Bastard Box" concept (a hybrid of the Elmer and Mercury chassis) was killed because it was ill-defined. The team pivoted to refining the "Elmer" chassis into "Zeus," grafting PCI extensibility onto legacy hardware to save design cycles.

#### Triage Protocol: "Surgical Strikes" and Standardization

The extreme time pressure exposed critical failures that required immediate, non-negotiable interventions ("triage"):

- **The "Dead Nuts Flat" Thermal Crisis:** Program management identified that the "Elmer" mainboard relied on an aluminum heat spreader contacting the chassis bottom. Because rapid sheet metal fabrication cannot hold "dead nuts flat" tolerances, there was a high risk of thermal runaway.
  - _Triage:_ The engineering team executed a "surgical strike" ECO (Engineering Change Order). They removed perforation holes under the heat spreader to maximize thermal mass for conduction and increased perforation density near the power supply to force convective airflow.
- **The Metric/SAE "Screw-Up":** A logistics failure on the initial "Elmer" prototypes resulted in a mix of metric and SAE hardware, which threatened to halt the assembly line during the final crunch.
  - _Triage:_ To eliminate the risk of cross-threading or tooling confusion on the line, the Lead Engineer enforced a draconian **4-40 UNC hardware standardization**, purging all metric fasteners from the "Zeus" assembly.
- **Manual Rework:** When digital handoffs failed (e.g., a "botched" enclosure), the team resorted to manual machining. In one instance, a perf top had to be manually machined by the model shop (Sputnik) to match a specific botched unit to ensure it was ready for the show floor.
