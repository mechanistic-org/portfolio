# Elmer / Zeus: converting a reference enclosure without inheriting its assumptions

## Project summary

- **Role boundary:** Contract mechanical design through Mechanistic: layout, enclosure changes, PCB retention, I/O-panel work, prototype release, and vendor interface. WebTV retained product definition, electrical design, component inputs, regulatory requirements, and program decisions.
- **Mandate:** Complete a Galaxy Home Network Server mechanical design, codenamed Zeus, by adapting the existing Elmer enclosure around a different component and I/O set.
- **Core result:** The record shows a deliberate keep/change/drop process rather than a blank-sheet enclosure. The proposal preserves Elmer's bezel and top where possible, remakes the base for new I/O, and the surviving artifacts record the resulting top, base, fan-bracket, thermal-interface, and connector decisions.

## Reuse was a constraint, not a shortcut

The November 2000 proposal states the architecture plainly: `Zeus will be based on the Elmer enclosure.` WebTV wanted to retain the bezel assembly and top cover unchanged if possible, but the I/O panel differed, so the base would have to be remade.

That conditional phrase - `if possible` - is the useful part. Reference designs save time only when every retained interface is requalified against the new system. A top cover may still fit the outer envelope while the base, rear boundary, PCB support, power supply, airflow path, and service hardware all change underneath it.

The proposal turns that uncertainty into work packages. It allocates 20 planned hours to kick-off and specification review, 40 to product design, and 40 to refinement and prototype work. The listed tasks include modeling primary components in Pro/ENGINEER, retaining additional PCBs, updating the I/O panel, specifying PCB placement, designing an Ethernet adaptor card, releasing the CAD database, working directly with prototype vendors, fabricating a check model, and verifying design integrity.

Those are planned hours and contracted deliverables. They are not proof of a 100-hour elapsed schedule or a fixed-price outcome.

## The mechanical worksheet became the interface map

The surviving `Portal Node Mech.xls` starts from a Mercury production enclosure and marks what would carry over. Some subassemblies are explicitly not used. The worksheet calls the bezel custom plastic and the chassis top and bottom custom sheet metal. It retains a 56 W power supply, a 45 GB drive, dampening grommets, fan isolation retainers, drive and top-cover foam, three CPU heat sinks, two thermal pads, and a custom aluminum heat spreader.

The I/O sheet then inventories the Elmer board boundary: RF modulator, RJ-11, USB, stacked S-video and RCA connectors, optical output, coax, smart-card connector, slide switch, and status LEDs. One coax specification is even marked uncertain. That note is small but important: the worksheet records an unresolved component identity instead of silently converting resemblance into equivalence.

Together, the two sheets are the real design instrument. They expose where a reference enclosure can be reused, where custom mechanics remain necessary, and where an electromechanical specification still needs confirmation.

## The artifacts verify authored geometry

Three preserved Pro/ENGINEER IGES logs were generated on 19 November 2000. One exports part `ELMER_TOP` into `zeus_top_11_19_00.igs`; another exports `ZEUS_BASE`; the third exports `FAN_BRACKET`. All three name `erik` as the user and record millimeter model space.

These logs provide a narrow but strong authorship boundary: Erik generated the preserved top, base, and fan-bracket geometry. They do not prove how many enclosures were fabricated, which event received them, or how the hardware performed in service.

## Identity and evidence boundaries

Elmer and Zeus are treated here as one program lineage. The proposal says Zeus was based on Elmer, and the top-cover export itself crosses the names: part `ELMER_TOP` becomes a `zeus_top` file. The former standalone `zeus` page is therefore an alias, not a second accomplishment.

The supporting WebTV email notebook is preserved locally, but its underlying email-archive custody remains unresolved. It informed search; it is not publication authority. Claims of a nine-day fabrication cycle, five server and twelve node deliveries, a $10,000 budget, zero thermal failures, 100-percent yield, universal 4-40 standardization, and trade-show operation have been removed because this pass did not find exact curated primary support.

No Elmer/Zeus image is selected in the active evidence store. The component worksheet and source trail carry the explanatory burden; a decorative gallery is not applicable for this pass.

## Quantified impact

- **Scoped** 100 planned hours across requirements, product design, refinement, and prototype work.
- **Separated** retained Elmer surfaces from a remade base and changed I/O boundary.
- **Mapped** enclosure, drive, power, thermal, fastening, and I/O decisions in one surviving worksheet.
- **Preserved** three same-day Pro/ENGINEER exports naming Erik as the geometry author.
- **Merged** the Zeus alias into the canonical Elmer/Zeus project identity.
- **Retired** unsupported schedule, quantity, budget, yield, and field-performance claims.
