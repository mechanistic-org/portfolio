Kinematic Forensic Analysis: Stall Detection
I. COMPONENT IDENTITY
• Sensors: Littelfuse 18ADS-5K; Honeywell SNDH-H Series.
• Type: Hall Effect Geartooth Speed Sensors (Flux Reversal / Zero Speed).
• Target: Ferrous Steel Gears (4-32 Pitch).
II. THE ANATOMY OF DETECTION (Heuristic Analysis)
Forensic reconstruction of the jam identification logic based on component capabilities.

1. The Physical Mechanism: Flux Reversal
   The sensors do not detect motion directly; they detect the disruption of magnetic flux.
   • Nominal State: As the drive train rotates, the ferrous gear teeth pass the sensor face. This modifies the flux density of the sensor's internal permanent magnet.
   • Signal Generation: This fluctuation triggers the Hall IC, generating a digital square wave output where frequency is directly proportional to rotation speed. The Littelfuse 18ADS specifically generates one pulse per tooth.
2. The Logic of Failure: The "Pulse Gap"
   The system identifies a mechanical jam not by what the sensor sees, but by what is missing.
   • The Command: The Beckhoff C6030 IPC sends a "Run" command to the motor (High Current).
   • The Feedback: The controller simultaneously monitors the Hall Effect sensor for a corresponding pulse train.
   • The Jam Signature: If the motor command is active (Energy = 1) but the sensor output remains static (Frequency = 0 Hz), the system registers a "True Zero Speed" event.
   • Inference: High current + Zero Pulses = Mechanical Stall/Seizure.
3. The "True Zero" Necessity
   Standard speed sensors fail at low RPMs (drop-out). The specified sensors (18ADS, SNDH-H) feature "True Zero Speed" capability. This is critical for the dispensing module because a jam often occurs at the start of a cycle or during slow, high-torque compression of ingredients. A sensor without zero-speed capability would be blind to a start-up stall; these sensors confirm the "stopped" condition instantly.

Tilt Ramp Actuation: Forensic Report
I. PROJECT SUMMARY
• Role: Lead Motion Systems Engineer [Project "Bender"]
• Mandate: Eliminate "parasitic load" on servo motors during static hold phases of the dispense cycle.
• Core Achievement: Implementation of a Tribological Lock (Self-Locking Worm Drive) to decouple load holding from electrical power consumption.
II. THE ANATOMY OF FAILURE (Heuristic Analysis)
Analysis based on SP17D020 and KHK Technical Data (09-worm-gears-technical.pdf).
• The Trigger (Crisis): Backdrive Torque & Thermal Runaway. When the "Tilt Arm" (Item 10: TILT_ARM_DRIVEN_2) positions a food container at an incline (to receive ingredients), gravity exerts a rotational force (moment) on the drive shaft. In a standard spur gear system, the motor must apply continuous holding torque to fight this gravity vector. This leads to:

1. Thermal Spikes: Motor coils overheat maintaining position (Zero Speed / High Torque).
2. Catastrophic Drift: Upon power loss (E-Stop or outage), the arm collapses, dumping the meal.
   • The Intervention (Fix): Geometric "Self-Locking" Architecture. We utilized a Worm Gear Pair (Worm + Worm Wheel) characterized by a specific geometry where the Lead Angle (γ) is less than 4°.
   ◦ Physics of the Fix: By selecting a single-thread worm (e.g., SW or KWG series) with a shallow lead angle (e.g., 3°35' or 3°11' as seen in KHK catalog specs), the system exploits friction. The coefficient of friction on the gear flanks exceeds the tangent of the lead angle.
   ◦ The Result: The worm wheel (connected to the load) creates a "locking" friction vector that prevents it from driving the worm shaft.
   • The Result (Impact): Zero-Energy Static Hold. The mechanism achieves a state where "the inability of worm wheels to drive the worms" becomes the primary safety brake.
