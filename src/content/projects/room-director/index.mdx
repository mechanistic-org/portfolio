# Project Sativa (Room Director) Forensic Report

## I. PROJECT SUMMARY

- **Role:** Head of Mechanical Engineering ("The Architect")
- **Mandate:** Engineer "Sativa" (Model N100), the premium "Room Director" switch featuring a chemically strengthened OLED touchscreen, Time-of-Flight (ToF) sensors, and Wi-Fi bridging to orchestrate the "layered lighting" ecosystem,.
- **Core Achievement:** Salvaged the EVT2 build from a **100% structural disintegration rate** (7/7 units) during Impact Testing by directing a "Full Circle Hinge" redesign, simultaneously resolving catastrophic cosmetic yield loss on the "VDI-15" painted housing,,.

## II. THE ANATOMY OF FAILURE (Heuristic Analysis)

### Thermal/Mechanical Crisis: "The Exploding Switch"

During EVT2 Impact and Drop testing, the Sativa unit suffered a total loss of structural integrity:

- **The Event:** A **100% failure rate (7 of 7 units)** occurred where the "Front housing and back cover separate" upon impact. The snap-fit architecture failed catastrophically, ejecting the high-voltage internals,.
- **The Rattle:** Concurrently, **7 of 7** units failed Micro Drop testing with "Can be hear the sound when shake the device," indicating internal battery and component loosening,.
- **Screen Fracture:** Drop testing resulted in "LCM crack" (Liquid Crystal Module) and Cover Glass fractures, rendering the $399 unit useless,.

### Quality/Supply Chain: "The Peeling Skin"

The premium "Fine Matte" aesthetic demanded by Industrial Design (ID) collided with manufacturing reality:

- **Adhesion Failure:** A **85% failure rate (6 of 7 units)** occurred for "Front housing corner coating peel off" during standard handling and environmental stress. The paint simply would not bond to the gloss substrate,.
- **Corrosion:** Reliability teardowns revealed "Screws get rusty" and "Bazooka metal material oxidize" inside the sealed enclosure after salt mist exposure (SAT-169),.

### The Fix: Structural & Surface Triage

I executed a multi-point engineering intervention to lock the chassis and secure the finish:

1.  **Structural Redesign (SAT-248):** Mandated a geometry change from a partial snap to a **"Full Circle Hinge."** Added reinforcement ribs and "removed the chamfer" to maximize contact area, locking the front and rear housings together. Validation samples immediately PASSED Drop, Crush, and Rapid Pull tests.
2.  **Surface Engineering (SAT-134):** Directed a tooling modification to alter the substrate finish from Gloss to **"VDI-15" (Satin)**. This increased surface energy for paint adhesion, eliminating the delamination defect,.
3.  **Adhesive Protocol (SAT-125):** Enforced the use of aggressive **3M 9453LE** adhesive and updated SOPs to secure the battery and eliminate the "shaker sound" rattle,.

## DEEP DIVE: The "Full Circle Hinge" (Structural Disintegration)

Based on the forensic engineering logs, the **"Full Circle Hinge"** was a structural intervention mandated to resolve a catastrophic disintegration failure identified as **SAT-248** ("Sativa & Elvis Front Housing Can Be Pulled off of Rear Housing").

During EVT2 Impact and Drop testing, the original design exhibited a **100% failure rate (7/7 units)** where the front housing physically separated from the back cover, often breaking the front boss,. The redesign solved this through three specific geometric alterations:

### 1. Geometric Closure (The "Full Circle")

The primary failure mode was the disengagement of the mating features during the high-G flex of an impact event. The original hinge design relied on an open or partial hook geometry that could deflect and release.

- **The Fix:** The Engineering Change Notice (ECN) explicitly directed a tooling modification to **"Change to a full circle hinge"**,.
- **The Physics:** By closing the geometry into a complete loop, the hinge permanently captured the mating axis. This prevented the clips from sliding out or deflecting open under vector forces generated during **Drop** or **Rapid Pull** tests.

### 2. Structural Reinforcement (The Rib)

Forensic photos showed that the "front boss is broken" during separation, indicating the plastic features were shearing under load,.

- **The Fix:** I mandated the addition of a **reinforcement rib** specifically "to strengthen the hinge structure",.
- **Material Addition:** The modification involved adding **0.30mm** of material bulk to the stress concentration points to prevent fracture.

### 3. Contact Maximization (Chamfer Removal)

The original tooling included a chamfer on the mating edge, likely for ease of assembly (draft), but this feature reduced the effective contact area, allowing the parts to "ramp" over each other during shock.

- **The Fix:** The directive was to **"Remove the chamfer"**.
- **The Result:** This squared off the engagement face, ensuring maximum vertical overlap and locking the front housing to the rear chassis.

### Validation Results

Following these tooling modifications, "mockup samples" were immediately subjected to regression testing:

- **Drop Test:** 3 of 3 samples **PASSED** immediately after reinforcement.
- **Strict Testing:** Even under "strict" drop conditions (multiple cycles on vulnerable angles), the structural integrity held significantly better, with only one minor failure observed after 4 cycles on a specific corner.

## DEEP DIVE: The "VDI-15" Intervention (Paint Peel)

Based on the forensic engineering logs, the transition to **VDI-15 texture** was a critical tooling intervention designed to arrest catastrophic paint adhesion failures (delamination) on the Sativa and Elvis user-facing housings.

### 1. The Failure: "The Peeling Skin" (SAT-127)

During EVT1 and EVT2 drop testing, the premium "Fine Matte" paint exhibited total adhesion loss.

- **The Defect:** "Front housing corner coating peel off" occurred at a rate of **85% (6 of 7 units)** during micro-drop testing,.
- **The Root Cause:** The original tooling utilized a **Gloss** or low-roughness finish (approx. 20% gloss) on the polycarbonate substrate (SABIC EXL9330),. This smooth surface provided insufficient surface energy and mechanical "bite" for the paint to bond, leading to separation upon impact.

### 2. The Fix: Surface Engineering (SAT-134)

I mandated a tooling modification to alter the substrate finish from Gloss to **VDI-15** (a specific EDM spark erosion texture standard).

- **Mechanical Interlock:** VDI-15 introduced a controlled micro-roughness to the molded part. This increased the effective surface area, allowing the coating to mechanically interlock with the plastic substrate, resolving the "peel off" delamination issue,.
- **Draft Angle Modification:** To implement VDI-15 without causing "drag marks" during part ejection, I directed the vendor (FIH) to increase the **draft angle** of the front housing surface to **3 degrees**.
- **Cost:** This specific Engineering Change Order (ECO) incurred a **$3,000** tooling modification fee.

## DEEP DIVE: High-Fidelity OLED Integration (JDM Governance)

Based on the forensic engineering logs, **The Architect** (Erik Norris) managed the high-fidelity OLED integration for the **Sativa** (Room Director) program by enforcing a rigorous JDM structure with **FIH (Foxconn)**, **Biel Crystal**, and **AUO**.

### 1. Strategic Component Selection & Cost Triage

To control the Bill of Materials (BOM) cost while maintaining the "Room Director" mandate, I directed specific architectural pivots regarding the display module:

- **The OLED Down-Selection:** We rejected the Futaba 1.3" OLED and the larger AUO 1.63" option, locking in the **AUO 1.45" AMOLED** as the Plan of Record (POR).
- **Cost Benchmarking:** This component was negotiated to a quotation of **$18 per unit**, benchmarked against market products like the "ZenWatch 2" to validate pricing leverage.
- **Risk Mitigation:** To validate the smaller form factor before final PCBs were ready, I mandated the fabrication of an "OLED Adapter Board" to bridge the 1.63" to 1.45" gap, utilizing a local California build to bypass JDM latency.

### 2. Glass Engineering & Yield Defense (Biel Partnership)

The integration of the **Corning 2320** (and alternative **Dragontrail**) cover glass required direct intervention to prevent "Pixel Perfect" cosmetic standards from destroying yield:

- **Material Swap:** We directed **Biel Crystal** to prepare **Dragontrail glass** samples as a cost/availability alternative to Gorilla Glass, requiring immediate drop-test validation to prove parity.
- **Yield over Bling (Logo Redesign):** The "metallic color" logo on the glass caused significant yield loss. I authorized a DVT design change to **remove the metallic finish** and switch to standard Pantone colors (PMS 2336 C / 405 C), prioritizing manufacturing stability over pure ID preference.

### 3. Assembly Architecture: The "Glue" Intervention

To ensure the high-cost OLED and glass stack survived the 10-year life mandate, I overhauled the assembly process:

- **From Tape to Chemistry:** I rejected the initial "adhesive tape" proposal for attaching the Cover Glass (CG) to the front housing, citing high reliability risk. Instead, I mandated a **liquid gluing process** for EVT1. Validation testing (Thermal Shock 168hrs) proved the glue bond held where tape would fail.
- **Stack Alignment:** Forensic reviews identified a "Misalignment between viewing area and active area." I ordered a shift in the Viewing Area (VA) dimension to synchronize the OLED active pixels with the glass masking.

## DEEP DIVE: The "Dead Front" Aesthetic (Optical Masking)

Based on the forensic engineering logs and cosmetic specifications, the **"Dead Front"** aesthetic (where the Sativa switch appears as a seamless, monolithic black slab when inactive) was achieved through a combination of optical filtering and advanced manufacturing techniques designed to hide internal components and seal light leaks.

### 1. IR Film: Masking the "Blue" Eyes (SAT-103)

The "dead front" illusion was initially broken by the Time-of-Flight (ToF) sensors, which were visible through the cover glass.

- **The Defect:** JIRA ticket **SAT-103** noted that the **"Sensor windows are too obvious"** and appeared distinct or "blue" against the black bezel, destroying the seamless look.
- **The Optical Fix:** The engineering team mandated the addition of **"IR plastic film"** between the cover glass and the ToF sensor,.
- **The Physics:** This film acted as a band-pass filter. It visually masked the internal components (making them appear black to the human eye to match the glass) while remaining transparent to the infrared wavelengths required for the proximity sensor to detect user presence.

### 2. Pad Printing / Stencil Modification: Sealing the Light (SAT-1601)

The Sativa's "3D formed glass" featured curved edges that created a critical failure mode for the "dead front" intent: light leakage.

- **The Defect:** "Edge light leakage" occurred at a rate of **90%** during development because standard printing stencils could not conform to the 3D curvature of the glass. This allowed the OLED backlight to bleed out the sides, revealing the seam.
- **The Fix:** To resolve this and the "saw edge" logo defects, the manufacturing process was shifted to utilize flexible stencils or **pad printing** (tampography) capable of applying the black masking ink deep into the curved regions. This ensured a hermetic light seal, guaranteeing that only the active pixels were visible.

## DEEP DIVE: Chemical Resistance (The "Kitchen Sink" Test)

Based on the reliability test plans and forensic engineering reports, the **Sativa** project was subjected to a rigorous battery of chemical resistance tests designed to simulate 10 years of exposure to household cleaning agents and personal care products.

### 1. The Chemical Agents

The test plan specified **12 Household Chemicals** and several harsh solvents that were applied to the device surfaces (Class A glass and Class B plastic housing).

- **Cleaners:** Dishwashing detergent, Pine Sol, Lysol, Clorox, 409, Pledge, Windex.
- **Cosmetics:** Hand soap, Shampoo, Conditioner, Hairspray, Toothpaste, Shaving cream.
- **Solvents:** Isopropanol (IPA), Petroleum Ether, Nail Polish Remover (Acetone).
- **Stressors:** Artificial Sweat (pH 4.5 & 8.0), Cooking Oil, Gasoline.

### 2. The Test Procedures

The protocols defined two distinct methods of exposure:

- **The Spot Test:** Apply >2ml of material for 1 hour. Criterion: No permanent staining.
- **The Wipe Test:** Wipe 200 times in 2 minutes with saturated gauze.

### 3. Documented Failures & Observations

- **Gasoline vs. Labels (SAT-1):** The regulatory labels failed the gasoline wipe test ("Label can't readable - 6/6"), forcing a material upgrade.
- **Nail Polish Remover vs. Finish:** Tests indicated that Acetone caused the "gloss surface [to] change rough" on plastic components, degrading the premium finish.
- **Sweat & Corrosion (SAT-169):** While not purely a surface finish failure, the introduction of sweat/humidity revealed that the **screws** and internal metal parts were susceptible to rust, leading to a "Found the rust inside" defect classification.
