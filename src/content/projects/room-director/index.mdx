CONTEXT: The following analysis details the structural, cosmetic, and functional deficiencies identified during the EVT/DVT cycles for the Room Director (Sativa), Extension Switch (Elvis), Base (Bazooka), and Faceplate (Waldo).
TONE: Objective, Technical, Root-Cause Focused.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. CRITICAL RELIABILITY FAILURES (The "Stop Ship" Risks)
1. The "Wet Hands" Ingress Failure (Chemical Resistance)
• Crisis: During DVT, units failed the "Wet Hands" simulation (Artificial Sweat) and chemical resistance tests. Exposure to these agents caused immediate gloss surface changes, roughness, and oxidization on the Bazooka metal contacts.
• Impact: Threatened the premium "Satin" finish and electrical safety.
• Resolution: Command override. The Architect directed the team to classify the test as "information only" and proceed with a FAIL result to preserve schedule velocity.
2. The "Floating Cap" Delamination (Elvis)
• Crisis: The "Elvis" extension switch exhibited a catastrophic "Plastic cap floating" failure mode where the top cosmetic cap separated from the housing.
• Root Cause: A dual failure of chemistry and geometry.
◦ Chemistry: The pressure-sensitive adhesive (PSA) was insufficient for the load.
◦ Geometry: A 0.1mm CAD mismatch existed between the internal adhesive pocket and the mating part, preventing proper contact.
• Intervention: Switched from PSA to structural glue (requiring new fixturing) and corrected the steel tool geometry.
3. Structural Dissolution (Drop & Crush Tests)
• Drop Test: Sativa and Elvis units suffered catastrophic disassembly during 1.3m drop tests. Failures included front housing/back cover separation, broken internal bosses, and cracked LCMs (Liquid Crystal Modules).
• Crush Test: Units failed to withstand compressive loads, resulting in adhesive gap separation and housing detachment.
• Micro Drop: Repeated low-height drops caused internal battery rattling due to adhesive failure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. COSMETIC & YIELD PATHOLOGY
1. The "Glass Yield" Crisis (Sativa)
• Defect: High reject rates for the Sativa Cover Glass (Lens).
◦ Edge Light Leakage: 90% failure rate due to imperfect masking.
◦ Scratches: 15% failure rate caused by collision between 3D glass parts during tray loading/transport.
◦ Logo Defects: 25% failure rate in printing the logo on the curved 3D glass surface.
2. Adhesive Residue (Waldo)
• Defect: Protective films applied to Waldo faceplates left stubborn glue residue upon removal, and in some cases, the film floated or bubbled before reaching the customer. This created an immediate "out of box" cosmetic failure.
3. Plating & Finish Degradation
• Bazooka: Black Zinc plating chipped away after multiple installation cycles, revealing the raw steel underneath.
• Anodization: "Bronze" anodized parts suffered from target mismatches (dL* -4.24), leading to 100% rejection of specific lots.
• Paint Adhesion: Front housing paint peeled off during thermal stress and abrasion tests.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. MECHANICAL & ASSEMBLY CONFLICTS
1. The "Label Lift" Debacle
• Issue: The main rear labels on Sativa/Elvis units lifted at the edges due to stiffness and weak adhesive (3M 467).
• Fix: Required an Engineering Change Order (ECO) to upgrade to 3M 9453LE (high-strength adhesive) and modify the label geometry to remove the Serial Number notch which acted as a stress concentrator.
2. Tolerance Stack-up & Fitment
• Interference: The "Bazooka" base and "Waldo" faceplate exhibited a "wiggle" and "zero-to-zero" interference issues, making installation difficult or leaving unsightly gaps.
• Stamping Limits: The Waldo chassis design contained sharp corners and hook gaps (\<2mm) that were impossible to manufacture via progressive die stamping, forcing DFM (Design for Manufacturing) geometry relaxations.
3. Electromechanical Dissonance
• Buzzing: Bazooka units produced audible buzzing noise.
• Shock Hazard: Electric shock risk identified when inserting the switch module into the base.
• Key Feel: The main switch button suffered from "jamming" and poor tactile feel (cheap sound) due to friction and interference, requiring the application of dry lubricant and grease.
IV. FIELD FAILURE (Post-Launch)
AMOLED Burn-In
• Latent Defect: Long-term users reported image retention on the 1.45-inch AMOLED display. The static text elements (e.g., scene names) caused uneven degradation of the organic diodes, a limitation of the display technology selected for a device with always-on elements.

What exactly caused the 'Wet Hands' failure during EVT2?

Forensic Engineering Analysis: The "Wet Hands" Failure
CONTEXT: During the EVT2 validation cycle, the "Wet Hands" protocol was designed to simulate accelerated environmental corrosion and chemical ingress. The failure was not mechanical but chemical and cosmetic, resulting from material incompatibility with the test agents under thermal stress.
DIRECT CAUSE: The failure was triggered by the application of Artificial Sweat (pH 4.5 & 8.0) and Nail Polish Remover. When combined with the test's environmental stress factors (65°C at 90% Relative Humidity for 24 hours), these agents chemically attacked the device's exterior finishes and metal components.
THE PATHOLOGY OF FAILURE:
• 1. Cosmetic Degradation (The Gloss Failure): The most prominent failure mode was a "gloss surface change" where the premium finish became "rough" after exposure. The chemical solvents in the artificial sweat and nail polish remover dissolved or etched the topcoat of the plastic housing (Sativa/Elvis), destroying the "Satin" cosmetic intent.
• 2. Metal Oxidation (The Corrosion): The saline content of the artificial sweat penetrated the "Bazooka" base unit, causing:
◦ Oxidation: The metal material on the bottom side of the Bazooka oxidized, leading to visible "color differences".
◦ Rust: Screws and internal metal connectors exhibited immediate rusting.
◦ Contact Degradation: The electrical contacts (DUT Connector) showed signs of oxidation, posing a risk to long-term electrical reliability.
FORENSIC CONCLUSION: The "Wet Hands" failure was a material science defect. The protective coatings and plating specifications were insufficient to withstand the corrosive interaction of saline (sweat) and harsh solvents (acetone in nail polish remover) when catalyzed by high heat and humidity. Rather than re-engineer the materials (which would delay PVT), the Principal Architect reclassified the test as "information only," effectively accepting the risk of cosmetic degradation in the field.
How did the OLED burn-in risk impact the design?

Forensic Engineering Analysis: The OLED Burn-In Pathology
CONTEXT: The decision to utilize a 1.45-inch AMOLED display for the Room Director was driven by a requirement for "True Blacks" so the device would vanish into the wall shadows when inactive. However, this aesthetic requirement created a direct conflict with the chemical limitations of organic light-emitting diodes (OLED), specifically their susceptibility to uneven degradation (burn-in) when displaying static UI elements like scene names ("Relax," "Everyday").
DESIGN INTERVENTIONS (Mitigation Protocols):
To combat the "10,000-hour burn-in threshold," the engineering team implemented a multi-layered defense strategy relying on sensor fusion and software behavior:
• 1. The "Auto-Wake" Logic (Sensor Fusion): The primary defense was to keep the screen OFF as much as possible. The device integrated a Time-of-Flight (ToF) sensor alongside a standard PIR motion sensor.
◦ Function: The ToF sensor allowed the device to distinguish between a user walking by (triggering only the low-level "Night Light" via the load) and a user approaching the switch (triggering the AMOLED display). This drastically reduced "pixels-on" time.
• 2. The "Star Field" Screen Saver: When the display was active but idle, the software employed a "Star Field" screen saver. This low-intensity, moving pattern prevented static pixels from remaining energized at high brightness, distributing the wear across the panel to avoid ghosting.
• 3. Brightness Normalization: Field data and forums suggested that blue organic pixels degrade fastest. To extend the half-life of the panel, the system encouraged brightness settings between 50-70%, sacrificing peak luminance for longevity.
FORENSIC OUTCOME: Despite these interventions, field pathology confirms that burn-in remained the dominant long-term failure mode. Post-mortem analysis of field units indicates that after approximately 10,000 to 16,000 hours of powered-on time, units exhibited visible "smudges" or ghosting of static UI text, a permanent chemical scar on the device.
