# Room Director: what two failed drop campaigns actually taught

## Project summary

- **Role boundary:** Locoroll-side mechanical design and reliability leadership for the Room Director enclosure and its mechanical interfaces. Product management, industrial design, electrical design, firmware, test execution, and manufacturing were shared across the client and supplier teams.
- **Mandate:** Develop a premium, touch-sensitive wall controller whose glass, coatings, housing, and internal retention could survive household exposure and a 1.3 m drop protocol.
- **Core result:** Reliability work produced a useful diagnosis rather than a clean success story. EVT1 and EVT2 both failed the drop campaign; a separate household-chemical campaign passed. The distinction matters because it prevents one test result from being used to imply another.

## The specification described a surface, not just a box

The draft reliability specification treats cosmetics as a functional requirement. It defines chemical exposures, abrasion methods, pass/fail criteria, and the expectation that household contact should not leave permanent staining or marring. That is the right frame for a wall controller: the glass and coating are not trim added after the mechanism. They are the primary human interface.

But a requirement is not a result. The draft is retained here to show what the program intended to protect; validation packets determine what the hardware actually demonstrated.

## One protocol, many failure modes

The EVT1 drop packet specifies a 1.3 m height, one drop on each of six faces and four corners, and hardwood or ceramic impact surfaces. It covers six Sativa and six Elvis samples and returns an overall failure. The observations are not one neat root cause: they include cracked Sativa cover glass and Elvis rattle, separation, and label problems.

That spread is the engineering point. A drop event travels through a system. Glass fracture, coating damage, snap or adhesive separation, internal rattle, and loss of function can share an impact but require different corrective actions.

The mechanical/test tracker preserves that decomposition. It records glass shatter, coating peel, front-substrate color, touch-panel lift, snap-hinge disassembly, rust, scratches, wear, and functional issues. The tracker is evidence of an active risk surface, not proof that every line closed.

## The second packet did not earn a victory lap

EVT2 repeated the 1.3 m protocol across six Sativa and seven Elvis samples. The packet again returns an overall failure. Sativa observations include cover-glass cracking and coating peel along with main-key, power, and functional failures; Elvis also retains a functional failure in the recorded set.

Earlier prose turned progression through EVT and DVT into production readiness. That inference has been removed. A later build can be more informative without being successful. Here, the value of EVT2 is that it tested another hardware state under a comparable method and still exposed unresolved mechanisms.

## Chemical resistance was a different result

The EVT1 household-chemical report covers fourteen Sativa and fourteen Elvis samples. Its method applies a range of household agents, holds parts for four hours at 25 C and then 72 hours at 45 C, and checks function and external appearance every 24 hours. The report returns a pass, with the recorded observations at level zero.

That pass does not erase the drop failures, and the drop failures do not convert the chemical campaign into a failure. Keeping the two outcomes separate is more useful than forcing them into a single heroic arc: the surface system passed one bounded exposure campaign while the impact architecture remained open.

## Evidence boundaries

The record supports mechanical and reliability leadership, but not sole ownership of the product or test organization. It also does not support the former claims of a wet-hands stop-ship override, a chemical-failure reclassification, 60-plus pull tests, a 20-plus custom-part count, production readiness, or complete failure closure. Those statements have been removed.

No Room Director image is selected in the active evidence store. Without a curated photograph or figure tied to one of these mechanisms, the visual instrument is not applicable for this pass.

## Quantified impact

- **Exercised** twelve samples in EVT1 and thirteen in EVT2 under the same 1.3 m face-and-corner drop method.
- **Preserved** both overall failures rather than converting schedule progression into a pass.
- **Separated** glass, coating, retention, rattle, and functional observations into distinct correction surfaces.
- **Verified** a separate household-chemical pass across fourteen Sativa and fourteen Elvis samples.
- **Retired** unsupported claims of stop-ship authority, production readiness, and complete resolution.
