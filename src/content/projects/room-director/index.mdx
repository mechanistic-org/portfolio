# Room Director: making the glass interface buildable

## A glass surface with a mechanical job

Room Director, known internally as Sativa, put a touch display behind a curved glass face on NOON's wall-mounted lighting controller. The apparent simplicity depended on a compact stack: cover glass and touch sensor, AMOLED display and flex circuits, molded front housing, and the rear enclosure and contacts. The front had to look continuous while leaving room for the display, sensors, bonding and mechanical retention.

My role was Locoroll-side mechanical design and reliability leadership for the enclosure and its interfaces. I worked across the product, industrial-design, electrical, firmware, test and manufacturing teams. FIH's assembly instructions, supplier drawings and test reports show the factory's part of that work; the engineering drawings identify individual authors where the record permits. Sativa shared development records with the Elvis extension switch, but its glass-and-display stack was a distinct mechanical problem.

The [May assembly instructions](/assets/room-director/full-pass-250/touch-assembly.webp) make that architecture visible. A Sativa-specific fixture places the touch module onto the front housing. Separate steps manage the OLED flex, gasket and internal connections. The glass could not be designed independently of the features and operations behind it. {/* ^[evidence:LK-069e42bb] ^[evidence:LK-fe9c8ace] */}

## Retaining the housing while clearing the display

The [February EVT2 hinge comparison](/assets/room-director/full-pass-250/hinge-change.webp) shows how small housing details carried different jobs. SAT-146 changes the snap hinge toward a full circular engagement, removes a chamfer and adds a strengthening rib. Another view adds paired features for restraint along the y-axis. These are changes to engagement and location, rather than a generic instruction to make the housing stronger.

The same report calls out a separate [0.50 mm front-housing change](/assets/room-director/full-pass-250/display-clearance.webp) to avoid interference with the AMOLED. That detail is specific to packaging the display. Increasing engagement at a hinge and providing clearance around a fragile module have to coexist in the same compact enclosure. A feature that improves one interface can consume space needed by another.

The figures document the proposed EVT2 changes and their stated purposes. They do not, on their own, establish which change caused a later test outcome. The report also includes Elvis-only dimmer-key work, which is separate from the Sativa geometry shown here. {/* ^[evidence:LK-b792e126] */}

## Bonding required a controlled assembly process

Touch-panel lift was tracked as SAT-114. By the May 8 action review, the team reported using glue between the touch panel and front housing in EVT2 for manufacturing stability. The entry records three samples passing a wood-floor drop check and three passing thermal shock, with FIH's Kelvin responsible for the item. Those were targeted checks of a particular correction.

The assembly instructions turn the bond into an operation: [dispense FC1682b along a defined path](/assets/room-director/full-pass-250/glue-process.webp), pre-fix the OLED flex, place the touch module with its fixture, then hold the front housing and module [in a screw clamping jig for at least five hours](/assets/room-director/full-pass-250/clamp-process.webp). The glue path, placement and clamped hold are all part of the assembly definition. Merely naming an adhesive would leave the process incomplete.

This is also why the targeted SAT-114 checks need to remain distinct from the complete-device drop campaigns. A bonded subassembly can pass its local check while another interface still rattles, cracks or loses function under a later test. {/* ^[evidence:LK-17e30be8] ^[evidence:LK-fe9c8ace] */}

## Defining the surface and how to inspect it

The glass needed an appearance reference as well as geometry. I am identified as the author and approver on the [041-00004 glass appearance specification](/assets/room-director/full-pass-250/glass-appearance.webp). Its June 21 revision defines mirror-polished glass with a black back-printed surface and references the Sativa glass part, 431-00004. The appearance specification and the physical part have different identities; keeping that distinction clear gives purchasing, manufacturing and inspection a common reference.

The drawing separates free-state flatness from dimensions controlled in the constrained condition after surface treatment. It also distinguishes first-article inspection, incoming inspection and marked capability dimensions, with video measurement as the default unless a feature requires another method. These instructions describe how a supplier should establish conformance, not just the nominal shape it should reproduce.

A May supplier-drawing redline makes a related distinction: split the glass part drawing from the touch-panel assembly drawing, and avoid duplicating the part dimensions on the assembly. The working drawing-review notes similarly call for functional inspection and correspondence between first-article reports and tolerance stacks. The handoff needed usable part identities, revisions and measurement instructions alongside CAD. {/* ^[evidence:LK-fe072066] ^[evidence:LK-e0ef484c] ^[evidence:LK-cf742d12] */}

The bronze finish also had its own reference. Drawing 041-00008, which likewise carries my authorship and approval, specifies metallic paint over the Sativa bronze PC/ABS reference and a VDI-15 texture. This is a different material-and-finish definition from the Elvis cap's matte-UV discussions in the shared tracker. {/* ^[evidence:LK-d0761c2f] */}

## A shared label problem with several possible fixes

The Sativa/Elvis rear-label review shows another interface that was easy to underestimate. Corners around the serial-number notch lifted, concentrating the problem at a small geometric interruption in the label.

The February discussion compares a stronger adhesive with changes to the notch, serial-number printing and label construction. FIH also discusses clamping and the practical constraints of producing the proposed labels. The report records one passed adhesive sample while two others were still under test. It is useful evidence of alternatives and supplier negotiation; the retained file does not establish a final validated label solution. {/* ^[evidence:LK-5349f8b4] ^[evidence:LK-958d2d0f] */}

## What the complete-device tests demonstrated

The main drop method used a 1.3 m height, six faces and four corners, over hardwood or ceramic. The January EVT1 packet covered six Sativa and six Elvis units and returned an overall failure. The April EVT2 packet covered six Sativa and seven Elvis units and also failed. Glass fracture, coating damage, retention, rattle and functional observations required separate follow-up; they were not interchangeable symptoms of one proven root cause.

The later DVT records extend that history. In the July 4 report, all six listed Sativa samples have rattle observations, and Sativa #27 also has an LCM-crack observation. Their function-test rows pass, while the report's overall result is FAIL. The [July 10 fixture photographs](/assets/room-director/full-pass-250/drop-fixture.webp) show the physical setup. On that date, a smaller set of two Sativa and two Elvis samples returns PASS: both Sativa units record OK for the ten drop directions and pass the function check. The DVT cover sheets state safety-focused acceptance criteria.

That smaller pass is a real result for the recorded samples. The available reports do not tie it to a complete as-built revision history or establish final qualification of every released unit. The record supports showing the progression, including its change in sample size, without assigning a causal victory to one housing or adhesive change. {/* ^[evidence:LK-06f48f7f] ^[evidence:LK-6e6a5752] ^[evidence:LK-f644dabb] ^[evidence:LK-542e0313] ^[evidence:LK-7625cbc3] */}

A separate household-chemical campaign passed across fourteen Sativa and fourteen Elvis samples, using four hours at 25 C followed by 72 hours at 45 C and periodic appearance and function checks. That result establishes performance under its own exposure method. It neither resolves nor inherits the drop campaign's failures. {/* ^[evidence:LK-07947e21] */}

## Engineering the interfaces through the handoff

The work connected a premium glass surface to the details needed to build it: housing engagement, display clearance, adhesive placement, fixtures, appearance references and inspection definitions. The test record then made it possible to discuss those interfaces against observed hardware behavior.

My involvement continued from my initial hiring through the company's dissolution and purchase by Savant. I was there with the team on launch day, ringing the gong. The contribution was mechanical design and reliability leadership within that shared effort, continuing beyond the individual test packets shown here. {/* ^[evidence:EV-1839de0e490c] */}

NOON publicly launched the system on October 26, 2017, with Room Director available separately and in the starter kit. The system received a 2018 Red Dot Product Design award, credited to NOON's in-house design team. Those are product and team outcomes; the test samples above do not establish shipment quantities or a complete final-build qualification. [NOON launch announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2017/10/26/1154179/0/en/Noon-Home-Inc-Brings-Professional-Quality-Layered-Lighting-Directly-to-Consumers.html), [Red Dot project record](https://www.red-dot.org/project/noon-23465/).

The later service history matters for a connected product. Savant's notice discontinued the standalone NOON app and cloud service on February 28, 2023, retaining local load control and support for systems integrated with Savant Pro. This describes the product's later service life, separate from my 2017 engineering and launch work. [Savant discontinuation notice](https://support.savant.com/noon/articles/en_US/Support_Article/Product-Discontinuation-Notice-NOON-Lighting-App/?c=Savant_Knowledge%3AIntegrations&fs=Search&l=en_US&pn=1).
