## I. PROJECT SUMMARY

- **Role:** Lead Mechanical Engineer / Industrial Design Consultant (Mechanistic / Jenerik Operations)
- **Timeline:** 2000 – 2002
- **Objective:** Architect the mechanical enclosure for "Galaxy" (UltimateTV), a next-generation high-definition satellite DVR and home media gateway.
- **Core Achievement:** Successfully engineered a consumer electronics chassis capable of dissipating a massive 150W thermal load (AMD K7 CPU) while maintaining strict EMI shielding and coordinating a rapid pivot to the Xbox console architecture upon program cancellation.

## III. CRITICAL MECHANICAL INTERVENTIONS (Ranked STAR Stories)

### 1. The 150-Watt Thermal Crisis

- **The Trigger:** The requirement to integrate a desktop-class AMD K7 CPU and three satellite tuners into a set-top box form factor.
- **The Tension:** Early thermal simulations indicated the system was generating 150W of power, threatening to melt internal components—specifically the Smart Card reader which hit 55°C.
- **The Intervention:** I engineered a chassis expansion (growing depth to 300mm) and designed a complex airflow path that intook air over the HDDs and exhausted through the PSU. I integrated a massive 50x60x30mm extruded heatsink and specified a high-performance Delta fan running at 2700 RPM.
- **The Result:** Achieved a CPU junction temperature of 84.0°C (within limits) and a total airflow of 28.3 CFM, saving the product from thermal failure.

### 2. The "Warping Base" Manufacturing Defect

- **The Trigger:** Vendor D&D Manufacturing flagged that our specification for "drawn standoffs" (integrated metal bumps for board mounting) would cause the chassis base to warp during the stamping process due to the quantity and placement.
- **The Tension:** A warped base would mean the motherboard wouldn't seat correctly, causing connector misalignment and potential shorting.
- **The Intervention:** I led a redesign of the chassis floor, adding "strengthening ribs" to stiffen the sheet metal and modifying the standoff details to "Hook 29" style to alleviate the stress accumulation.
- **The Result:** Preserved the low-cost sheet metal strategy while ensuring structural integrity for mass production.

### 3. The "Impossible Assembly" CPU Puck

- **The Trigger:** During the "DEV" build, we realized the CPU assembly could not be dropped into the main chassis as a pre-assembled unit due to fastener interference with the fixed partition.
- **The Tension:** This design flaw threatened to make the unit impossible to manufacture on a high-volume assembly line.
- **The Intervention:** I executed a rapid redesign of the "CPU Puck," breaking it down into sub-components that could be assembled _in situ_. I simultaneously redesigned the EMI flanges to ensure contact continuity despite the segmented assembly.
- **The Result:** Resolved the assembly bottleneck and improved EMI shielding effectiveness by moving to a 12.5mm hex hole pattern for better RF containment.
