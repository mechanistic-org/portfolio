Forensic Analysis: The "Hook 29" Stress Relief Intervention
SUBJECT: Chassis Base Yield Failure & Remediation COMPONENT: Base Pan (P/N 210-01007-001) MATERIAL: 0.91mm Cold Rolled Steel (CRS)
I. THE FAILURE MECHANISM (Stress Accumulation)
The initial engineering specification called for "drawn standoffs"—integral mounting bosses created by stretching the steel sheet to form a raised, threaded attach point for the motherboard.
• The Physics of Failure: The stamping process required to create these drawn forms introduced significant internal surface tension. Because of the high density and specific placement of these standoffs, the material stress accumulated rather than dissipating, causing the chassis base to warp and twist upon ejection from the progressive die. This threatened to make the chassis unusable for automated PCB assembly.
II. THE "HOOK 29" SOLUTION (Stress Relief)
Fabrication vendor D&D Manufacturing (specifically Chuck Pokonosky) rejected the initial design and mandated the implementation of the "Hook 29" detail (specifically "Form D Hook 29").
• The Mechanics of the Fix: Unlike the drawn standoff, which relies on stretching material, the Hook 29 detail utilizes a lancing or bridging process. By cutting (lancing) and forming the metal bridge, the process breaks the surface tension of the sheet steel, effectively relieving the internal stress that caused the warping.
• The Trade-Off (Exposure): The engineering compromise was sealing. While drawn standoffs are hermetic (sealed), the Hook 29 lancing process created small openings in the chassis floor, which the vendor noted would "allow exposure to the inner workings of the chassis".
III. STRUCTURAL COMPENSATION
To ensure the stress-relieved base maintained the necessary structural rigidity to support the 150W thermal load and heavy components (transformers, HDDs), the engineering team simultaneously implemented strengthening ribs across the chassis floor. This combination—Hook 29 for flatness and embossed ribs for stiffness—stabilized the part for mass production.
