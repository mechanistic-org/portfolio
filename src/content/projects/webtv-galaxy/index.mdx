# WebTV Galaxy

## Project summary

- **Role boundary:** Independent mechanical-design consultant responsible for mechanical design and Pro/ENGINEER documentation under WebTV direction. Industrial design, thermal analysis, electronics, fabrication, program management, and the wider product outcome remained cross-functional responsibilities.
- **Mandate:** Develop the mechanical package for the planned Galaxy Home Network gateway, whose requirements included three satellite tuners, dual hard drives for DVR storage, HDTV output, and picture-in-picture operation.
- **Evidence-bounded result:** The record documents a detailed prototype and DEV candidate with an evaluated thermal model and an active mechanical hitlist. It does not establish shipment, cancellation, production validation, or a causal transfer into Xbox.

## Thermal model

The April 23, 2001 consultant record reports a simulation run at 150 W total power, 45°C ambient, and 300 mm enclosure depth. With the modeled fan at 2700 RPM and 28.3 CFM, it reported an 84.0°C CPU junction, an 89.3°C Nemo device temperature, a 52.8°C hard-drive case temperature, and 55.0°C air near the smart-card reader.

Those values are simulation inputs and results, not measured production performance. The Fireball lct20 datasheet lists a 55°C operating maximum, which makes the modeled 52.8°C drive case temperature a narrow analytical comparison rather than proof of production margin. The fan's 37 dB value is a component specification, not a measured system acoustic floor. Moving the smart-card reader closer to the inlet remained a recommendation in the reviewed record; no later closure record proves the move was completed.

## CPU assembly and EMI direction

The July 27 DEV hitlist records CPU fasteners near a fixed partition and states that the CPU subsystem could not be dropped into the main assembly as a preassembled unit. Its required direction was an assembly built in parts. The same packet calls for EMI flanges all around and a better seal.

That evidence supports an assembly and shielding direction, not a verified endpoint. The page therefore does not claim completed 360-degree containment or a passed emissions result.

## Chassis and manufacturability

The surviving metal-parts packet proposes standoffs instead of dome forms, while the hitlist says base stiffening still required review and records other geometry as unresolved. It does not contain the former public story that named "Hook 29" forms, established a warped-base rescue, or proved flat parts at production stamping rates. Those claims have been retired rather than inferred from incomplete manufacturing discussion.

## Governance and evidence

The proposal defines Mechanistic as an independent contractor providing product-development and technical-consulting support, with a specific scope for mechanical design and Pro/ENGINEER documentation. It also places that work under WebTV direction and describes industrial design as a separate input. The dated hitlist distributes actions across a broader program team, reinforcing a cross-functional role boundary.

One April 20, 2001 invoice documents 142 hours of Galaxy support from March 7 through April 20 at $100 per hour, for a total of $14,200. It is one bounded invoice, not evidence of a recurring monthly burn rate or an allocation across three programs.

NotebookLM remains a research surface rather than publication authority. The dated local reconciliation accounts for all 35 sources, 34 notes, and 23 Studio audio artifacts. Ten audio records contain attribution-biased instructions, and none is used as claim authority. The reconstruction explicitly unions the 91-row declared `webtv_galaxy` archive with the 27-row dedicated `2001_WebTV_Receiver_galaxy` archive; neither silently supersedes the other.

## Program outcome and Xbox boundary

The reviewed Galaxy packet does not establish that the product shipped, that every hurdle closed, why the program ended, or an exact end date. It also does not document a technical handoff that made Galaxy the missing link to Xbox, saved Xbox, or transferred Galaxy's thermal architecture into that program. The records show company and planning adjacency. Adjacency is not causation, and later Xbox redesign work belongs to its own evidence scope.

## Quantified evidence

- **Modeled** a 150 W total load at 45°C ambient in a 300 mm enclosure.
- **Reported** 28.3 CFM, 84.0°C CPU junction, 52.8°C hard-drive case temperature, and 55.0°C air near the smart-card reader in that simulation.
- **Documented** a DEV assembly direction that built the CPU subsystem in parts around a fixed partition.
- **Tracked** all-around EMI-flange, seal, chassis-stiffness, and component-placement actions without converting them into unrecorded closures.
- **Invoiced** $14,200 for 142 Galaxy support hours in one bounded March 7 to April 20 period.
