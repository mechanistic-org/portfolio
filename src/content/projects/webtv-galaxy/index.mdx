### Galaxy / UltimateTV Forensic Report

**Source:** NotebookLM
**Extracted:** October 26, 2023

#### I. PROJECT SUMMARY

- **Role:** Lead Mechanical Architect / Principal Consultant (Mechanistic / Jenerik Operations)
- **Objective:** Integrate desktop-class computing power (AMD K7 CPU, 3 Tuners, HDD) into a consumer electronics form factor for the "Galaxy" Home Network Gateway.
- **Core Achievement:** Successfully engineered a chassis capable of dissipating a critical **150W thermal load** within a constrained 300mm footprint while maintaining EMI integrity and assembly feasibility.

- **Erik Lincicum (Norris):** Lead Mechanical Engineer (Mechanistic).

#### III. CRITICAL MECHANICAL INTERVENTIONS (Ranked STAR Stories)

##### 1. The 150-Watt Thermal Crisis

- **The Trigger:** April 23, 2001. Thermal simulations confirmed the AMD K7 processor and triple-tuner architecture generated a **150W total power load**, pushing internal ambient temperatures to critical levels.
- **The Tension:** Initial "Mercury-like" form factors were insufficient. The Smart Card reader location hit **55.0°C**, threatening component failure and user safety.
- **The Intervention:** I engineered a chassis depth expansion to **300mm** to support a high-velocity airflow tunnel. I integrated a massive **50x60x30mm extruded heatsink** and specified a Delta fan running at 2700 RPM. The airflow path was re-architected to intake over the HDD and exhaust through the PSU.
- **The Result:** Validated CPU junction temperatures at **84.0°C** (safe operating range) and achieved **28.3 CFM** total airflow, preventing a program-killing thermal failure.

##### 2. The "Warped Base" Manufacturing Defect

- **The Trigger:** June 20, 2001. D&D Manufacturing flagged a critical flaw in the stamping design. The specification of "drawn standoffs" for motherboard mounting created excessive stress in the sheet metal base.
- **The Tension:** The vendor warned of severe **warping** during the stamping process, which would result in motherboard misalignment, connector failure, and unusable chassis yield.
- **The Intervention:** I authorized a rapid redesign of the chassis floor, replacing the drawn standoffs with a "Hook 29" detail and adding specific **strengthening ribs** to counteract the material stress.
- **The Result:** Stabilized the chassis geometry for high-volume progressive die stamping, ensuring flat mating surfaces for the PCB assembly.

##### 3. The "Impossible Assembly" CPU Puck

- **The Trigger:** July 27, 2001. The "Mechanical Design Hitlist" revealed that the CPU "puck" assembly could not be installed into the main chassis as a pre-assembled unit due to fastener interference with the fixed partition.
- **The Tension:** This geometric conflict rendered the unit **unable to be assembled** on a production line, threatening the "DEV" build schedule.
- **The Intervention:** I executed a modular redesign of the CPU enclosure, breaking it into sub-components for in-situ assembly. I simultaneously upgraded the EMI shielding strategy, implementing **EMI flanges** and a **12.5mm hex hole pattern** to maintain RF containment despite the segmented assembly.
- **The Result:** Resolved the assembly bottleneck while enhancing EMI shielding effectiveness, allowing the DEV build to proceed on schedule.
