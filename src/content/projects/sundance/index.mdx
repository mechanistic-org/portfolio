# Sundance: isolating four front-access drives in 2RU

## Project summary

- **Role boundary:** Senior mechanical design engineering for the enclosure and front-drive mechanism. Industrial design, electrical design, firmware, validation, sourcing, and manufacturing remained cross-functional responsibilities.
- **Mandate:** Translate a proposed four-drive server into a 17-inch-wide, 2RU, 10-inch-deep enclosure with user-accessible drives, two 60 mm fans, and a 15 CFM cooling target.
- **Core result:** The HDD rail became an explicit isolation mechanism. The task record calls for full elastomer enclosure with no plastic contact points and a return to shoulder screws; the assembly BOM records sixteen shoulder screws, four per drive.

## The rail was the project

The requirements proposal made front serviceability non-negotiable. All four 3.5-inch drives were to come out through the front without removing the server from a rack. The same proposal allowed only 3.438 inches of height and 10 inches of depth, while reserving space for a custom main board, two 60 mm fans, and the airflow needed around two stacked rows of drives.

That turned a seemingly small plastic part into a system boundary. The rail had to guide a removable drive, retain it, avoid transmitting spindle vibration into the enclosure, and coexist with the front bezel and airflow openings.

## Tooling exposed the commitment

The surviving plastics comparison prices four tools. The HDD rail, P/N 520-1219-00, is the largest commitment in that sheet: $37,500 in tooling, a $15,000 40-percent down payment, a $2.23 quoted piece price, and a 45-day tooling lead. The other listed tools carry 30- or 35-day leads.

Those numbers are a quote, not evidence that the tool was ordered or that the product shipped. They still explain why the rail deserved design attention before release: a late isolation change would sit inside the longest quoted tool schedule in the comparison.

## From contact risk to a defined mechanism

The January task record is unusually direct. For the HDD rail it assigns Erik two changes: `modify to fully enclosed with elastomer (no plastic contact points)` and `revert to shoulder screws`. Nearby tasks coordinate the rail with the bezel opening, blanking plate, and HDD frame, showing that the mechanism was being resolved as part of the whole front-drive stack rather than as an isolated molding.

The assembly BOM closes the geometric intent into countable hardware. P/N 323-1210-00 is a 6-32 shoulder screw with a 5/32-inch-diameter, 3/16-inch-long shoulder. Quantity is sixteen, annotated `4 per drive`. The same BOM records two 60 mm fans and eight HDD rails across the four-drive assembly.

The useful engineering story is therefore not a generic claim about damping. It is the conversion of an acoustic contact risk into a repeatable constraint system: elastomer around the drive, no direct plastic contact, and four shoulder screws locating each drive.

## Evidence boundaries

The requirements document calls itself a proposal, so its storage capacities, enclosure dimensions, and environmental targets are presented as design intent. The tooling sheet is treated as a quote. The task tracker and BOM establish assigned changes and assembly definition, but they do not establish final production yield, launch timing, acoustic test performance, or field reliability.

Earlier prose claimed a 618-unit cosmetic inspection, 204 rejects, a 33.08% failure rate, rogue-rivet rework, a 2,000-unit ramp, and a two-week prototype-tooling result. No exact curated primary record supporting those claims was found in this pass, so they have been removed.

No Sundance image is selected in the active evidence store. A decorative gallery would add confidence without adding proof, so the visual instrument is not applicable for this pass.

## Quantified impact

- **Packaged** four front-access 3.5-inch drives in a proposed 17 x 3.438 x 10 inch envelope.
- **Resolved** the HDD rail around an explicit no-plastic-contact requirement.
- **Defined** sixteen shoulder screws, four per drive, in the assembly BOM.
- **Bounded** the production-intent rail against a quoted $37,500 tool, $2.23 piece price, and 45-day lead.
- **Preserved** two 60 mm fans and a 15 CFM target as requirements, not measured performance.
