# D-Command (Danko)

## Project summary

- **Role boundary:** Lead Mechanical Engineer with a documented solo mandate for part and assembly drawings. Electrical design, software, industrial design, sourcing, regulatory execution, supplier production, and the wider launch remained cross-functional responsibilities.
- **Mandate:** Engineer the mid-format Danko console on D-Control's architecture under aggressive cost-down reuse. The mechanical record spans more than 24 unique sheet-metal parts, nine PCB dimensional-control drawings, the plastics set, and the assembly drawings.
- **Core result:** The program recovered bowed fader pans, validated the fanless thermal arrangement, closed conductive-coating and EMI gaps in the plastic top covers, and passed the failed AC-withdraw test. UL Notice of Approval arrived April 19, 2005, nine days before the April 28 first customer ship.

## The anatomy of failure

### Vegas Mode thermal stress

The August 2004 test record exercised every motorized fader while nearly every LED was lit, with the unit flat on a table to restrict the bottom intake. The internal "attic" rose from 31°C to 41°C and the LED-supply heatsink from 56°C to 61°C. The supplies were rated for 50°C ambient, so the absolute heatsink temperature is not presented as a temperature-margin figure.

The response was process discipline rather than a hardware redesign. Manufacturing instructions kept the internal cable bundle away from the power supplies so it could not block the natural-convection path, and the user guide carried the airflow warning. The console remained stable through forty minutes of stress. The record also ties the lower heat load to a single-color LED architecture with 25% fewer emitters and half the driver chips.

### Fader-pan yield and green light bleed

PEM insertion stretched the sheet metal and bowed the first fader-pan lot; 21 of 42 parts were rejected. Immediate containment used 100% manual flatness inspection and table-edge cold work to recover usable stock. The unnamed vendor then changed its insertion control and added a flattening operation. The record supports containment and correction, not a claim that the response produced a permanent zero-defect endpoint.

At the same time, chassis contact with LCD flex cables stressed solder joints and leaked green light into mute switches. Relief cuts in the subassembly cleared the flex cables instead of accepting the interference as an assembly variation.

### Plastic enclosure and EMI closure

Danko used injection-molded ABS top covers where D-Control used steel. Conductive coating turned the plastic into part of the shielding system, but early coating buildup in button holes caused sticking. A revised coating and masking process was verified below 4 ohms corner-to-corner without button friction.

The closure chain remained specific: ECO 6531 added copper foil to bridge coated plastic to chassis ground; ECO 8114 released the missing EMI gasket; routing and gasket sealing addressed leakage near the RJ45 port. Slot geometry had been controlled in CAD and the enclosure passed its first pre-scan without a geometry change. The final system passed FCC Class A, CE, and Canadian emissions.

### AC-withdraw compliance

Pre-production units failed the UL AC-withdraw test because voltage on the unplugged mains pins did not fall below 60 Vdc within two seconds. The AC-harness bleeder changed from 1 MΩ to 750 kΩ. Production then moved to a line filter with a built-in bleeder, removing the manual crimp. The plastic and PCB records also maintained a UL 94 V-0 material requirement.

UL issued its Notice of Approval on April 19, 2005. The first customer ship followed on April 28. Those anchors support the nine-day compliance-to-ship interval; the surviving date fields do not support an exact total project duration, so none is claimed here.

## Governance and evidence

The mechanical-documentation assignment concentrated the part and assembly drawing work in one engineer while D-Control sustaining work continued. That is recorded as a single-point governance risk inside a cross-functional program, not as sole ownership of the product or launch.

Two bounded logistics results remain in the accepted record: 100 feet of internal snake cable was eliminated per unit through routing changes, and a single $5,000 expedite fee was waived against accepted schedule slack. No vendor is named, and no broader tooling-fee claim is made.

NotebookLM remains a research surface rather than publication authority. The dated local reconciliation accounts for all 36 primary sources, 29 notes, eight primary audio artifacts, five arbitration sources, and four arbitration-audio records. The former public briefing is no longer linked because its generation instructions encouraged unsupported attribution. Local preservation satisfies custody without carrying that drift into the public page.

## Quantified impact

- **Recovered** usable parts from a lot in which 21 of 42 fader pans were rejected, while the vendor corrected its process.
- **Validated** stable operation through a forty-minute Vegas Mode stress test with a 61°C LED-supply heatsink reading.
- **Verified** the conductive coating below 4 ohms corner-to-corner without button friction.
- **Closed** the failed AC-withdraw test by changing the bleeder from 1 MΩ to 750 kΩ.
- **Eliminated** 100 feet of internal cable per unit and **negotiated** one $5,000 expedite-fee waiver.
- **Reached** first customer ship nine days after UL Notice of Approval.
