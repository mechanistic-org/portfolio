### D-Command (Project Danko) Forensic Report

#### I. PROJECT SUMMARY

- **Role:** Mechanical Engineer
- **Objective:** Architect and stabilize the mechanical chassis and enclosure for "D-Command," a mid-sized tactile control surface derived from the flagship D-Control architecture.
- **Core Achievement:** Engineered emergency recovery protocols for critical sheet metal yielding issues (pan bowing) and plastic injection failures (end-cap fracturing), rescuing the April 2005 First Customer Ship (FCS) date.

#### II. THE CAST (Team & Stakeholders)

- **Erik Norris:** Mechanical Engineer (Lead on Danko Mechanicals)
- **Industrial Design:** NewDealDesign (Chris Lenart) / Pivot / Modus
- **Neal Breitbarth:** Mechanical Engineering Manager (Escalation point, DFM guidance)
- **Al Siy:** QA/Manufacturing (Incoming inspection & vendor auditing)
- **Fidel Saucedo:** Vendor Engineer, Mass Precision (Sheet metal fabrication partner)
- **Thomas Oiwa:** Manufacturing Engineer (ECO implementation & assembly fixtures)
- **Vicky Moreno:** Field Service/Manufacturing (Reporter of field failures)
- **Terri Merrell:** Purchasing/Manufacturing (Vendor coordination)

#### III. CRITICAL MECHANICAL INTERVENTIONS (Ranked STAR Stories)

##### 1. The Fader Pan "Bowing" Crisis

- **The Trigger:** During pre-production, Fader Module pans arrived significantly warped ("bowed") from the vendor. The hydraulic press used to insert PEM hardware was stretching the material at each insertion point, creating a cumulative arch.
- **The Tension:** Units could not be assembled without extreme force. Field installers reported needing "ratchet straps" to compress the Main and Fader units together to align screw holes. Installers were resorting to placing shims under the feet to "hold it together".
- **The Intervention:**
  1.  **Immediate containment:** Deployed to the manufacturing floor to train assembly staff on a manual "tweaking" protocol to counteract the press-induced bow.
  2.  **Root Cause Correction:** Directed Mass Precision to implement localized distortion compensation.
  3.  **Tolerance Relief:** Redesigned the rear tie-plate brackets (**ECO 8000**) to ovalize mounting holes, allowing lateral float between the Main and Fader units.
- **The Result:** Eliminated the need for field shims and ratchet straps. Restored chassis alignment to allow consistent 0.02" gaps between modules.

##### 2. The Sticky Button Crisis (Process Hack)

- **The Trigger:** Fader buttons were sticking in the "down" position.
- **The Failure:** The hardware insertion process was physically deforming the plastic fader pan, bowing it enough to pinch the buttons.
- **The Intervention:** Invented a "Post-Hardware Flattening" operation—a mandatory extra manufacturing step at Mass Precision to physically compress the bowing back into tolerance after insertion.
- **The Result:** 100% elimination of sticky buttons without retooling the mold.

##### 2. The End Cap Fracture Event

- **The Trigger:** Plastic side caps were cracking at the mold line/weld line during installation. The mounting holes were undersized relative to the boss, causing screw heads to bottom out against the structural foam and exert hoop stress on the part wall.
- **The Tension:** High-visibility cosmetic failure on a premium product. Incoming inspection yield dropped to less than 40% (5 out of 10 sets passed).
- **The Intervention:**
  1.  **Design Change:** Executed **ECO 6310** to increase the diameter of mounting holes and internal bosses, reducing lateral stress on fasteners.
  2.  **Tooling Mod:** Authorized T1 tooling modifications with PPI Plastics to relieve interference fit.
- **The Result:** Yield restored to acceptable levels; new parts validated via "T1 approval" faxed to vendor.

##### 3. UL/EMC Compliance Failure (AC Withdraw)\*\*

- **The Trigger:** 48 hours prior to shipping, the unit failed the UL "AC Withdraw" test. Voltage did not drop below 60 Vdc within 2 seconds.
- **The Tension:** "Line Down" scenario. Regulatory hold prevented shipment of finished goods inventory.
- **The Intervention:** Engineered a retrofit solution involving the addition of a specific EMI gasket (PN 9440-13240-01) to the assembly. Pushed an urgent **ECO 8114** to modify the BOM and assembly prints immediately.
- **The Result:** Unit passed UL re-test; Notice of Approval (NOA) received, clearing the product for USA, Canada, and EU shipment.
