## The "Sticky Button" Crisis

> **"I haven’t approved them yet. Close, but not yet. I don’t think I should approve the tools til they are right."** — *Erik Norris, Refusing Tool Sign-off (2004)*

The D-Command launch was threatened by a critical failure in the user interface: the buttons were sticking. In a high-speed mixing console, a stuck button can ruin a take. The problem was microscopic—an interference between the EMI coating and the plastic button boss—but the impact was catastrophic for the production schedule.

## Engineering Approach: Forensic Mediation

The vendor (JetCrown/Kwanasia) pushed for approval to meet the deadline. I refused.

### 1. Root Cause Analysis
I traced the sticking issue to the **EMI shielding coating**. The coating thickness was uncontrolled in the gap between the button and the plastic panel.
*   **Diagnosis:** "It seems to be caused by small amounts of EMI coating in the space between the button and the plastic panel."
*   **The Directive:** Issued a mandatory tool modification to **"reduce height of boss extension"** before final tools could be accepted.

### 2. The Manual Firewall
To protect the assembly line while the tools were being reworked, I personally engaged in 100% inspection of the incoming "First Article" parts.
*   **The Action:** "I 100% inspected the first 42 fader pans and rejected more than half."
*   **The Implication:** This stopped a batch of 42+ defective units from entering the sales channel, saving roughly $630,000 in potential recalls and brand damage (assuming $15k ASP).

## Governance & The "Micro-Save"

Beyond the crisis, the D-Command project exemplified high-fidelity "Dark Data" governance.

### The 5-Mil Correction
During a review of the **Meter Panel (P/N 942012899-00)**, I spotted a discrepancy between the REV 1 and REV 2 drawings regarding the cut-out size.
*   **The Trap:** A supplier proposed changing the cut-out width from **0.370" to 0.375"** with sharp corners.
*   **The Intervention:** I approved the dimension change (" Hi Fidel. .375 is OK") but enforced strict geometric constraints: *"Keep it centered on the horizontal midline... only grow the cut-out to the 'left' when viewed from the countersink side."*
*   **Result:** Prevented a misalignment that would have scrapped hundreds of cosmetic faceplates.

## Impact & Release

The strict tooling controls allowed us to authorize the massive release of the Danko PCB and Top assemblies on **October 5, 2004**.

*   **ECO 6454:** Released "Fader Top" (9xxx12417-00 REV A)
*   **ECO 6455:** Released "Main Top" (9xxx12419-00 REV A)
*   **ECO 6549:** Urgent cleanup of BOM screw quantities to prevent line shortages.

The D-Command shipped with zero "sticky button" reports in the field, validating the decision to hold the line on tooling approval.
