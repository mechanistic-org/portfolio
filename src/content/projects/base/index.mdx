# Project Bazooka (Base) Forensic Report

## I. PROJECT SUMMARY

- **Role:** Head of Mechanical Engineering ("The Architect").
- **Mandate:** Engineer "Bazooka" (Model N190), the high-voltage (120V) universal base station responsible for 650W-850W power delivery, phase angle control, and the structural docking of user-facing modules (Sativa/Elvis) within a JDM partnership (Bel Power Solutions).
- **Core Achievement:** Salvaged the program from a **100% mechanical retention failure rate** (7/7 units stuck) and critical "Wiggle" instability by directing a structural overhaul to the "3Tabs concept" and enforcing a safety-critical redesign of the Air-Gap switch mechanism.

## II. THE ANATOMY OF FAILURE (Heuristic Analysis)

### 1. Structural Crisis: The "3Tabs" Intervention

The **"3Tabs concept"** was a critical structural redesign mandated to resolve catastrophic interface failures between the user-facing switches and the high-voltage base.

<Admonition variant="danger">
	**The Trigger:** JIRA Ticket **SAT-60** tracked excessive "Wiggle" between
	modules. Concurrently, retention testing showed a **100% failure rate** (7/7
	units), where modules became "stuck in Bazooka" and could not be removed.
</Admonition>

- **The Fix:** We replaced the over-constrained latching geometry with a deterministic 3-point engagement system. I directed the vendor (FIH) to redesign the inner structures to ensure "only single tab acts as 'stop' in Y," eliminating the mechanical conflict (jamming) while removing the wiggle.
- **Result:** The redesign forced a "line down" tooling modification (Nov 7, 2016) but successfully passed subsequent crush, drop, and retention validation.

### 2. Fitment Crisis: The 0.3mm Slide-Lock Failure

A rigid tolerance stack-up analysis revealed a critical **-0.30mm interference** in the "drop and slide" installation mechanism.

<Admonition variant="info">
	**The Math:** While nominal design showed 0.00mm gap, tolerance loops (±0.13mm
	variations) resulted in a negative clearance where the chassis tabs would
	physically collide with the plastic body, preventing installation.
</Admonition>

- **The Tooling Defect:** Compounding this, the vendor used "Large square dimples" for spacing, which caused **"pillowing"** (deformation) of the surrounding steel, eating further into the tolerance margin.
- **The Solution:** I mandated a tooling change to replace the square stampings with **"smaller circular pips"**, eliminating the stress-induced warping. We also increased the chassis slot lengths to absorb the tolerance stack, ensuring smooth engagement.

### 3. Safety Crisis: The "Air-Gap" Override

The 850W dimming architecture required a **UL 1472** compliant "Mechanical Air Gap Switch" to physically cut 120V power during bulb changes.

<Admonition variant="caution">
	**The Failure (BAZ-81):** The ID-driven cosmetic button design failed to
	consistently make electrical connection and risked shock hazards. The "Stop
	Ship" order reverted the design to a **"functional square 'TEST' geometry"**.
</Admonition>

- **The Intervention:** This prioritized positive mechanical actuation over aesthetics, ensuring the switch survived the required 100-cycle short-circuit test.
- **Design Impact:** This forced a PCB layout change to accommodate the new switch mechanism and a structural update to add a "button contactor guide" directly to the PCB for rigid reference.

## III. THE GROUNDING CRISIS (Safety & Continuity)

The "Bazooka" grounding architecture relied on a hybrid connection: a hardwired earth ground and a mechanical bond to the **Waldo** chassis. This interface faced critical failures during validation.

### 1. Conductivity vs. Corrosion (BAZ-41)

The mandate **"Must maintain conductivity between Bazooka and chassis"** required removing non-conductive finishes from mating surfaces.

- **The Failure:** Manufacturing "Laser etched away area [was] grossly too large," exposing excessive raw metal.
- **The Risk:** This created a trade-off: insufficient etching risked shock hazards (isolation), while excessive etching invited **"Oxidation of chassis surface"** (Rust) during High Temp/Humidity testing, leading to a "poor grounding surface."

### 2. The Tribology Conflict (WAL-10)

The grounding design collided with the cosmetic finish of the chassis.

- **The Defect:** The **Black Zinc** plating on the chassis suffered from **"coating chips away after multiple Bazooka installs"** (WAL-10).
- **The Impact:** The flaking zinc (a sacrificial anode) created conductive debris and left the underlying SPCC steel vulnerable to rapid oxidation, degrading the low-impedance path required for UL safety compliance.

### 3. Compliance Friction (BAZ-90)

**BAZ-90** flagged a basic compliance failure: **"Ground wire color."** The initial harness specification failed to meet regional/UL color coding standards (Green/Yellow), necessitating an immediate BOM change to ensure regulatory passage.

## IV. VENDOR MANAGEMENT (JDM Partnership)

**Bel Power Solutions** (The Power Expert) was critical to stabilizing the high-voltage architecture.

- **The Dynamic:** I managed the relationship with **Craig Tuffrey** (Ops Manager), balancing Locoroll's aggressive industrial design geometry with Bel's conservative safety margins.
- **The Win:** We successfully packaged the 120V dimming circuitry within the "Bazooka" cylinder without compromising the **UL 1472** air-gap requirements, effectively creating a miniaturized power plant that fit into a standard wallbox.

## V. GOVERNANCE & RHYTHM (The Waterfall)

I imposed a strict **Waterfall Strategy** to align the complex JDM supply chain.

- **Executive Gates (0-2):** Locked funding and resource allocation, critical for the expensive high-voltage tooling.
- **Functional Check Points (CP1-CP4):**
  - **CP3 (Architecture):** Identified the "Wiggle" risk.
  - **CP4 (Validation):** The "Meat Grinder" where reliability testing exposed the **Grounding/Oxidation** risks (BAZ-41).
  - **Line Down Tracking:** Weekly reviews with **Bel Power** and **FIH** (Kelvin/Nick) ensured the **11/09** tooling release date was met despite the "3Tabs" redesign.

## VI. LINKEDIN ARTIFACTS (The Numbers)

1.  **Redesigned** the entire module-to-chassis interface (3Tabs) to eliminate 100% of "Wiggle" and "Retention" failures (7/7).
2.  **Orchestrated** the forensic resolution of the **BAZ-41** grounding crisis, balancing laser-etching processes against environmental corrosion risks to ensure UL safety compliance.
3.  **Managed** a JDM partnership with Bel Power Solutions to deliver a certified 120V dimming architecture in a constrained cylindrical volume.
4.  **Enforced** a "Round Pip" tooling modification (via interference analysis) to prevent deformation in the slide-lock mechanism.
5.  **Delivered** the "Bazooka" Base station on schedule (11/09 Tooling Release) as the foundational power node for the entire Noon Home ecosystem.
