### [SC48 "Lux" / "Capaldi"] Forensic Report

#### I. PROJECT SUMMARY

- **Role:** Lead Mechanical Engineer / Industrial Designer
- **Objective:** Architect an "Embedded Live Sound" console integrating a DSP engine and PC motherboard into a touring-grade chassis under a strict less than $15k COGS cap.
- **Core Achievement:** Solved a critical **75°C** thermal shutdown failure by re-architecting the chassis from 3U to 4U, while simultaneously eliminating expensive aluminum extrusions to meet aggressive cost targets.

#### II. THE CAST (Team & Stakeholders)

- **Neal Breitbarth:** Mechanical Manager (My direct report; resource allocation).
- **Marc Schuman:** Hardware/Thermal Engineering (Partner in thermal matrix testing and sensor data analysis).
- **Stan Cotey:** Product Marketing (Defined the "Profile" aesthetic requirements).
- **Terri Merrell:** Senior Buyer (Managed vendor relations with VTech/Jetcrown).
- **VTech (China):** Contract Manufacturer (CMS) for final integration.
- **Jetcrown:** Plastics Vendor (Tooling and injection molding).

#### III. CRITICAL MECHANICAL INTERVENTIONS (Ranked STAR Stories)

##### 1. The "Meltdown" Mitigation (Thermal Shutdown)

- **The Trigger:** During "Capaldi FOH" environmental chamber testing, the embedded CPU temperature hit **75°C**, triggering an automatic system shutdown.
- **The Tension:** The product is a live sound console; a thermal shutdown during a performance is a catastrophic, "show-stopping" failure mode. The density of the embedded PC motherboard (Kontron 986LCD-M) inside the console created a heat trap.
- **The Intervention:** I executed a "Design of Experiments" thermal matrix, testing chassis heights (3U vs 4U) and fan voltages (5.6V to 12V). I re-architected the chassis to a **4U layout** and implemented a high-velocity cooling solution using **three 80mm fans at 12V**.
- **The Result:** The intervention dropped the internal temperature rise to **22.6°C** (Left side), keeping the CPU well within the safe operating zone and eliminating the shutdown risk.

##### 2. The "No Extrusion" Mandate (Cost Engineering)

- **The Trigger:** To meet the aggressive COGS target for the "Embedded Live Sound" market, Finance/Management issued a hard constraint: "There will be no extrusions". Previous consoles relied on expensive aluminum extrusions for structural rigidity.
- **The Tension:** A touring console must withstand significant vibration and shock. Removing the extrusion frame risked creating a "floppy" chassis that would fail drop tests or warp during transport.
- **The Intervention:** I engineered the "4U Rake Back I-Beam" (P/N 9420-58317-00), a complex folded sheet metal spine that mimics the structural properties of an extrusion using lower-cost steel fabrication methods.
- **The Result:** Achieved touring-grade rigidity using only standard sheet metal (Mass Precision/VTech), keeping the mechanical cost contribution to **20% of COGS** while maintaining the "Profile" family aesthetic.

##### 3. The Embedded PC Integration

- **The Trigger:** The requirement to embed a standard Kontron Mini-ITX motherboard into a proprietary appliance chassis without exposing consumer PC ports to the user.
- **The Tension:** standard ITX form factors conflict with console ergonomics, and direct rear-panel access was blocked by the chassis geometry.
- **The Intervention:** I designed custom PCI brackets (P/N 9420-58318-00) and a riser system to mount the motherboard internally, routing IO to custom panel-mount connectors on the exterior.
- **The Result:** Delivered a seamless "appliance" experience where the user interacts with "Console IO" rather than "Computer Ports."
