## Making the integrated console fit

SC48 brought the host computer, audio processing and local I/O into one live-sound console. I was brought in to work across its architecture, design, engineering and validation, and get it into manufacturing quickly. I led mechanical engineering and industrial design: the enclosure, thermal packaging, sheet-metal and plastic parts, and the physical interfaces between them. Electrical design, software, validation, sourcing and contract manufacturing were shared responsibilities.

The work was to keep those systems compatible while the design was changing. More room for cooling could change electromagnetic behavior. A side cover could change the airflow that had worked on an open test unit. Moving a control on the surface could move a connector or mounting hole on the board beneath it. The development record connects those decisions from the integrated-console brief to prototypes, tests, interface drawings and fabrication packages.

## Reuse set the packaging problem

The February 2007 requirements preferred processing and I/O inside the console, while allowing a separate rack if integration proved impractical. The COGS target was below $15,000. Mechanical assumptions called for side cooling and no aluminum extrusions. Those were design targets and assumptions, not achieved production figures.

By June, a high-reuse plan placed a Mini-ITX host and PCI extender alongside mix engines, Hub48, audio I/O, control-surface electronics and power distribution. The companion board-breakout document was explicitly preliminary: it identified board sizes and stacked processing boards while the top-surface electronics were still poorly defined. Existing electronics gave the project a starting point, but their dimensions, connectors and cooling needs still had to work together inside the console.

The archive's 4U rake photographs make that challenge tangible. Beneath the sloping surface are rear board stacks, intervening metalwork, ribbon cables, power wiring and host hardware. These are photographs of development hardware, not proof of a final production arrangement. Paired with the early block diagram, they show how an architecture became a physical assembly that could be investigated.

## The enclosure was also part of the experiment

The November thermal files record an active investigation. Notes describe tilting plenums, taping an opening, moving a cable, changing vents around the ADCs and changing fan voltage during acquisition. The summary collects 17 configuration labels, with 28 populated Left/Right readings. These are not 17 independent controlled experiments.

One 4U comparison uses three 80 mm fans and measures left R69. The source marks the 8.5 V record **stopped early/estimates**; the pair does not establish steady-state qualification.

| Ambient | 24.3°C | 22.6°C |
| Left R69 rise | 61.3°C | 51.4°C |
| Left R69 absolute | 85.6°C | 74.0°C |

The late-November report describes another 4U arrangement, with one 80 mm and one 120 mm fan, as thermally and acoustically acceptable. Preliminary electromagnetic testing still found significant noise with the power supply under the ADCs. Additional sheet metal was needed to continue EM work. Temperature had answered one part of the packaging question and exposed the need to keep investigating others.

## Lower profiles and different airflow paths

December testing returned to 3U. Four-fan crossflow worked without the filter and side cover, but did not work with them fitted. Three internal 80 mm fans running front-to-back worked at 12 V and 8.5 V; preliminary EM behavior was better than the 4U arrangement, while acoustic verification was still due. A hybrid with a 3U back and 4U front was also built and reported as looking promising.

The run traces preserve the progression within those tests. The December 5 crossflow file marks a change to 12 V at 4500 seconds and added vents at 6500 seconds. The December 7 hybrid file marks 8.5 V followed by 12 V at 3000 seconds. The plotted channels show different responses, with ambient retained separately. Other abrupt transitions have no matching explanation in the notes and remain unattributed. They are visible parts of the experimental record, not grounds to invent an intervention or a clean causal gain.

The summary also deserves care. Some selected values occur in several workbooks, some setup labels conflict with the headers, and six readings do not exactly reproduce any searched 14-channel run row. Four have partial matches with unexplained numerical differences. That limits a numerical ranking of all configurations. It does not prevent explaining the documented progression: crossflow, front-to-back and hybrid arrangements were investigated against thermal, acoustic and electromagnetic behavior together.

We ultimately chose 4U because it worked. SC48 passed EMI certification and shipped. The retained source record does not establish the exact shipped fan/filter revision or final acoustic result.

## Airflow became a set of parts

The airflow work continued into fabrication definition. The BOM revisions describe die-cut, scored and formed LDPE ducts, a reticulated-polyurethane filter and a separate PS/CPU seal. The renders show folded guidance surfaces and flat patterns, while quotation drawings define the shrouds for sourcing. These are useful views of how a flow path becomes parts with edges, attachments and surrounding interfaces; a render by itself is not measured airflow.

The packaging definition also considered cable passages. Retained renders show a wire guide with rectangular openings and a separate seal with narrow slits. The cost records list an injection-molded guide and die-cut rubber seal, but their quantities change from two each in REV3 to zero in REV6. These are documented options, not an identified part of the shipped airflow arrangement.

The BOM uses 9420-58856-00 and 9420-58857-00 while preliminary quotation drawings use 9440-58856-00 and 9440-58857-00. No approved crosswalk has been established. Their related descriptions and geometry support discussing the duct family, but they do not establish one final production kit or justify silently changing either identity.

February reporting records the duct/filter package going out for quotation, corrected metal documentation delivered for sourcing, and continued work on motherboard placement and CPU ducting. A quote, a prototype drawing and a released production assembly are different states in that process.

## Surface decisions reached the boards

The control surface governed more than appearance. A January keepout overlay prohibited through-hole components beneath a marked region. Later that month, control-surface PCB drawing work was waiting for the final surface arrangement. This is the physical dependency that made layout coordination necessary, not evidence of a collision in a built unit.

At the back, power and computer connections occupied the space between the audio I/O banks. The retained layout views show two power inlets side by side in one arrangement and stacked in others, with computer connections in the same central zone and ventilation above. These views make the rear-panel packaging problem visible; they do not establish which arrangement was manufactured.

By February, I was incorporating revised artwork into CAD, equalizing top-panel margins and printing the layout at full scale for Product Marketing. Work with the electronics team aligned board outlines, connector locations and controls. The initial PCI-extender DCD had already gone to Portwell in December; the main-left and main-right board documents followed in February.

The interface work extended beyond the two main control-surface boards. A main-I/O drawing was already marked for review in October 2007. In April, a power/reset drawing relocated the buttons and added a 26-pin header. These are interface-document changes; the inspected records do not establish which revisions reached manufactured hardware.

Their revision tables show the interface work continuing. Main-left updates followed artwork and layout, deleted a mounting hole, moved a 10-pin connector and moved three mounting holes by April 7. The video-board drawing records a connector-placement update on April 8. Main-right was updated to match layout on April 10. Those are dated drawing actions, not evidence of final system release or my personal handoff date. They show how changes at the surface and connector interfaces reached the controlled mechanical definition.

## Plastics, access and manufacturing definition

The outer parts developed alongside the internal interfaces. Three plastic directions were presented for review; the selected direction used left and right side covers and a front bolster without a rear trim piece. Work continued on the ACS encoder ring, the mouse-tray slot in the side cover and the relationship between the bolster and CD drive.

March reports preserve the alternatives rather than compressing them into one finished design. Full-size and slimline-CD bolsters were prepared for SLA evaluation. A second encoder design was completed and a third developed. By late March, the third encoder version had gone to quotation and prototype work, and the slimline CD had been selected for further detailing. The component renders show those part families, while exact revision and finished appearance need their own evidence.

The no-extrusion assumption directed the structure toward fabricated metal. A prototype 4U rake-back I-beam drawing defines a folded, perforated section with a 1.5 mm material note; it is marked prototype, prepared by pd and checked by en. That record explains a structural approach without turning every drawing into my work or a prototype into the shipped chassis.

Cost models and quotes supported this definition work. The sheet-metal piece-part model totals $521.46 plus $40 contingency, $561.46 in all. It is a model, not achieved system COGS. CNC and hard-tool scenarios remain separate, and the Profile benchmark's extrusion category is not a demonstrated SC48 saving. Supplier feedback and successive quotes informed detailing without establishing a final supplier/process outcome from quotation paperwork alone.
