# SC48 (Lux / Capaldi)

## Project summary

- **Role boundary:** Lead mechanical engineering and industrial design for the enclosure, thermal architecture, sheet-metal and plastic-part definition, and mechanical interfaces. Electrical design, software, validation, sourcing, and contract manufacturing remained cross-functional responsibilities.
- **Mandate:** Package the host computer, DSP hardware, control surface, and I/O into one live-sound console under a sub-$15,000 COGS target. The requirements preferred all processing and I/O inside the console; the cost assumptions separately mandated side cooling and no extrusions.
- **Core result:** A 17-configuration thermal study drove the enclosure from 3U to 4U. In the directly comparable 4U rows, three 80 mm fans at 12V reduced the left R69 temperature rise by 9.9°C and its absolute reading by 11.6°C versus 8.5V. A folded-steel I-beam provided the taller structure without adding the extrusion cost carried by the Profile benchmark.

## The anatomy of failure

### Thermal crisis: the 75°C kill event

The thermal record contains an explicit failure line: `temperature is 75C, system shutdowned.` The surviving log does not identify a BIOS setpoint beside that line, so 75°C is reported here as an observed shutdown, not a documented threshold specification.

The constraint stack was unusually tight. Product requirements called for the host and I/O to be integrated into the console; the COGS assumptions said cooling would enter from the sides; and the Kontron 986LCD-M manual required forced cooling within a 0-60°C operating-ambient envelope. The console therefore had to move air across a dense internal assembly without a separate processing rack.

The engineering response was empirical. Seventeen logged configurations varied chassis height, fan size, fan voltage, and rake geometry. The resulting architecture moved from 3U to 4U, used three 80 mm fans at 12V, and added die-cut, scored, and formed LDPE ducts to guide airflow.

The corrected comparison is the pair of 4U, three-80 mm-fan rows. At 8.5V and 24.3°C ambient, the left R69 rise was 61.3°C and the absolute reading was 85.6°C. At 12V and 22.6°C ambient, the rise was 51.4°C and the absolute reading was 74.0°C. The intervention therefore changed the recorded rise by 9.9°C and the absolute reading by 11.6°C. Earlier prose incorrectly called 22.6°C an internal rise, treated a margin-table value as a rise, and derived unsupported headroom; those claims have been retired.

### Structural crisis: the no-extrusion mandate

The 4U thermal architecture needed a wider, taller load path just as the cost assumptions declared: `There will be no extrusions.` The inherited Profile comparison showed why. Extrusions accounted for $121.70 per unit, or 6.8% of that control-surface rollup.

The SC48 sheet-metal packet includes the 4U Rake Back I-Beam, P/N 9420-58317-00. Its folded section created the structural spine using fabricated steel. The cost workbook kept CNC and hard-tool options explicit rather than collapsing them into a single production claim.

The CNC sheet-metal model totaled $521.46 across 38 line items plus a $40 contingency, for a $561.46 piece-part total. The Profile comparison is retained as a benchmark, not presented as SC48's total system cost: Profile sheet metal was $366.29 of $1,801.29, or 20.33%.

### Embedded-computer integration

The Kontron motherboard sat behind a dedicated 4U back panel and PCI frame, P/N 9420-58318-00. Separate left and right I/O brackets, P/Ns 9420-58326-00 and 9420-58327-00, carried the mechanical interfaces at the enclosure boundary. The BOM independently identifies the internal Ethernet extension, P/N 9180-26139-00.

This record supports the physical architecture - a commercial embedded motherboard packaged behind console-specific steel and cable interfaces. It does not support the former claims of zero I/O failures, vibration isolation, or a field-replaceable engine block, so those statements have been removed.

### Cosmetic shell and tooling

The plastics ledger distinguishes three parts: left side cover 9440-58841-00, right side cover 9440-58842-00, and front bolster 9440-58843-00. It assigns $36,000 of tooling to the left side cover and no separate tooling charge to the right, supporting a shared-tool interpretation without claiming a second invoice was literally avoided. The front bolster carries a separate $30,000 tooling entry.

The selected visual set contains the right side cover and front bolster. The earlier page mislabeled 9440-58842-00 as both side caps and overstated unsupported texture and assembly details; the captions below now follow the part and cost records.

## Quantified impact

- **Observed** a hard shutdown at 75°C in the surviving thermal log.
- **Tested** 17 thermal configurations across chassis and fan variables.
- **Reduced** the comparable left R69 rise from 61.3°C to 51.4°C and the absolute reading from 85.6°C to 74.0°C by moving the three-fan 4U row from 8.5V to 12V.
- **Modeled** the CNC sheet-metal piece-part total at $561.46 per unit, including a $40 contingency.
- **Removed** the $121.70-per-unit extrusion category present in the Profile benchmark from the SC48 architecture.
- **Released** a 26-drawing sheet-metal packet alongside a 17-document PCB design-control packet.
