# KSYSTEM-120 / Orpheus

## Project summary

- **Role boundary:** Mechanical design and NPI contribution. The record directly attributes schedules, integration actions, FAI procedure authorship, and specific part-level first-article records to Erik Norris. Industrial design, electrical and PCB design, firmware, sourcing, manufacturing execution, and certification remained cross-functional.
- **Mandate:** Develop and integrate the Orpheus chassis, removable-drive architecture, front-panel package, thermal and EMI features, and manufacturing documentation toward validation and pilot/ramp readiness.
- **Evidence-bounded result:** The archive establishes detailed product definition, mechanical integration, supplier coordination, and bounded first-article samples. It does not establish the former paint-throughput, Glow-performance, PCB-fallout, stop-ship, compliance-pass, volume-ramp, or shipment results.

## Product and archive identity

The December 11, 2008 rear-label record is explicitly titled 'KSYSTEM-120', while the BOM and assembly material name the mechanical program 'Orpheus'. Together they bind the public product identity to the development name without relying on generated synthesis.

The ratified '2009_03_Kaleidescape_Mini_System' vault contains 81 rows. Every one has the same source hash in the broader 6,123-row Kaleidescape locker. That broader locker includes 998 rows under an Orpheus folder, so the dedicated vault is a bounded project projection rather than the complete Orpheus corpus.

## Mechanical integration

Dated status records show Erik distributing the mechanical schedule and coordinating a build kit for early design validation. They assign him work around mechanical-evaluation PCB retention, CPU-heatsink mounting clips, feet and heatsinks, EMI/RFI gasket prototypes, and a rear-shield keepout/tab review. The same records distinguish functional prototypes, near-production builds, pilot-usable parts, and required changes before production.

The BOM resolves the package into a chassis base, PCB bracket, fan tray, power-supply enclosure, drive cage, removable-drive parts, front controls, feet, gaskets, wiring, and the KSYSTEM-120 rear label. The plastics packet identifies the Orpheus button, door, bezel, side-panel, drive-guide, and foot assemblies. These records support mechanical product definition and integration work, not sole ownership of every subsystem.

## Glow evidence boundary

The archive contains a dedicated Orpheus Glow-tuning presentation. Its extracted text identifies tuning resistor values and documents the PCB as constrained by the frame and inner panel. That is enough to retain Glow tuning as a design activity.

The current authoritative extract does not preserve the legacy 50-to-500-ohm change, 40x desensitization, 7-6-5-4-3 positional matrix, 60% visual-acceptance statement, or a shipped hardware-deterministic result. Those numbers and outcomes therefore remain outside the candidate.

## First article and manufacturing state

The mechanical FAI procedure lists Erik Norris as author of its draft and initial peer-review release. In the Orpheus sample records, Erik is named as originator for:

- fan mounts: 10 received, 10 inspected, 0 rejected;
- AC-tunnel parts: 18 received, 17 inspected, 0 rejected.

Those are bounded part-sample facts. They do not prove whole-product yield, production qualification, or regulatory compliance.

The supplier NPI packet contains a 50-piece first-lot ramp row while mass production remains a later state in the tracker. This supports pilot/ramp preparation and the 'validation' lifecycle classification. It does not establish a completed 300-unit ramp, series production, an 800,000-unit annual application, or shipment.

## Compliance and outcome boundary

EMI gaskets, grounding, shielding, and related prototype actions appear throughout the BOM and mechanical status records. Component datasheets and later adjacent-product launch material also contain compliance language. No curated KSYSTEM-120 whole-product test report establishes an Erik-owned FCC, CSA, acoustic, or thermal pass, so none is claimed.

Likewise, the local primary record does not substantiate the legacy 3-to-20-unit paint-masking result, 566% throughput increase, 20% PCB-fallout recovery, three simultaneous stop-ships, hammered-lid shipment, drilled-out grounding springs, Rev 5/Rev 7 mismatch, or $0.38-per-unit thermal decision.
