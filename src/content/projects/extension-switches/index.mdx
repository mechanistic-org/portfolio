# Project Elvis (Extension Switch) Forensic Report

## I. PROJECT SUMMARY

- **Role:** Head of Mechanical Engineering ("The Architect")
- **Mandate:** Engineer "Elvis" (Model N130), the wireless extension switch designed to replace standard 3-way switches and communicate with the Room Director, featuring a tactile dimming rocker and premium polycarbonate construction.
- **Core Achievement:** Salvaged the EVT2 build from a **100% thermal-mechanical failure rate** (7/7 units) by diagnosing a critical CAD geometry mismatch and mandating a shift from PSA tape to liquid structural glue to secure the $79 SKU.

## II. THE ANATOMY OF FAILURE (Heuristic Analysis)

### Thermal/Mechanical Crisis: "The Floating Cap"

During EVT2 Thermal Stress testing (65°C to -20°C), the Elvis unit suffered a catastrophic structural failure designated as "Plastic Cap Floating."

- **The Event:** A **100% failure rate (7 of 7 units)** occurred where the user-facing cosmetic cap physically separated from the front housing, leaving the switch inoperable and exposing internal seams.
- **The Forensic Find:** Analysis of **JIRA SAT-238** revealed a **0.1mm CAD geometry mismatch**. The internal pocket depth was actually **0.3mm** against a design intent of 0.2mm. The specified 0.25mm Nitto 57120W PSA tape was physically incapable of bridging the gap.
- **The Fallout:** The adhesive "did not withstand the crush test," leading to immediate separation upon handling.

### Quality/Supply Chain: Acoustics & Adhesion

- **The "Cheep" Sound:** Users reported a "Cheep sound" (SAT-145) and hollow acoustics when pressing the main switch. The internal housing volume acted as a resonance chamber, compromising the premium feel.
- **Label Lift:** The main label suffered edge lift due to a "Serial Number notch" in the outline, creating a stress concentration point. The vendor (FIH) claimed printing on the 0.15mm label was impossible, complicating serialization.
- **Paint Failure:** Early units failed "Pencil Hardness" and "Abrasion" tests, resulting in "Cap paint scratch" defects (SAT-171).

### The Fix: Structural Triage

I executed a multi-vector engineering intervention to secure the assembly:

1.  **Adhesive Overhaul (SAT-149):** Issued a "Stop Ship" on PSA tape. Mandated a transition to **liquid structural glue**, requiring the fabrication of custom clamping jigs ($15,000 investment). Modified units immediately **PASSED** Drop, Crush, and High Temp/Humidity tests.
2.  **Acoustic Dampening (SAT-281):** Directed the addition of **structural ribs** to the front housing to extend towards the cap, dampening the "hollow" acoustic signature.
3.  **Geometry Correction:** Modified the tool to eliminate the 0.1mm pocket depth error.
4.  **Label Redesign:** Eliminated the SN notch and forced the supply chain to adopt a **print-on-demand** serialization workflow, removing the stress point causing delamination.

## III. GOVERNANCE & RHYTHM

- **The Pulse:** The project followed a strict **Waterfall Strategy** with Functional Check Points (CP1-CP4), utilizing JIRA as the forensic log for "Line Down" issues like **SAT-149** (Adhesive Failure) and **SAT-238** (Gap Mismatch).
- **The Artifacts:**
  - **JIRA Pulse:** Ticket **SAT-149** became the central repository for the adhesive crisis, tracking the failure of Nitto tape and the validation of liquid glue.
  - **CMF Specifications:** **Locoroll CMF V16** defined the "Satin VDI 15" finish to resolve gloss-related paint adhesion failures.
  - **Action Logs:** Detailed tracking of tooling lead times, e.g., "Check tooling lead-time when ME drawing lock down".

## IV. LINKEDIN ARTIFACTS (The Numbers)

1.  **Recovered 100%** of EVT2 units from catastrophic "Plastic Cap Floating" failure (7/7) by engineering a liquid-adhesive structural intervention.
2.  **Scaled** Elvis production builds from **19** P1 prototypes to **720** DVT validation units to support rigorous environmental stress testing.
3.  **Resolved 0.1mm** mechanical interference in the adhesive pocket by enforcing forensic CAD analysis and tooling modification.
4.  **Eliminated** "Cheep sound" acoustic defects in **100%** of units by designing internal structural ribbing to dampen resonance.
5.  **Targeted** a Gross Margin of **$49** on a $79 MSRP (Gen 2) by optimizing the mechanical BOM and manufacturing processes.

## DEEP DIVE: The "Floating Cap" (Adhesive Failure)

Based on the forensic engineering logs and issue trackers, the transition to **liquid structural glue** was a "Stop Ship" intervention mandated by The Architect (Erik Norris) to salvage the **Elvis** (Extension Switch) from a catastrophic thermal-mechanical failure mode.

The liquid glue improved reliability by physically bridging a critical tooling error and providing a bond strength that exceeded the capabilities of the original Pressure Sensitive Adhesive (PSA) tape.

### 1. The Root Cause: "The Floating Cap" (SAT-238)

During EVT2 Thermal Stress testing (cycling between 65°C and -20°C), the Elvis units exhibited a **100% failure rate (7 of 7 units)** where the user-facing plastic cap separated from the housing, a defect labeled **"Plastic Cap Floating"**.

- **The Geometry Mismatch:** Forensic analysis revealed a **0.1mm geometric error** in the injection mold. The internal adhesive pocket depth was **0.3mm**, but the design intent was **0.2mm**.
- **The Failure:** The specified **0.25mm Nitto 57120W PSA tape** was physically too thin to bridge the 0.3mm gap effectively. Under thermal contraction, the tape lost contact, causing the cap to "float" or fall off.

### 2. The Intervention: Liquid Structural Bonding (SAT-149)

I rejected the PSA tape solution and enforced a transition to liquid glue to secure the assembly.

- **Gap Filling:** Unlike the fixed-thickness tape, liquid glue could expand to fill the irregular 0.3mm void, creating a solid, void-free interface between the cap and the housing.
- **Bond Strength:** Reliability testing proved that the liquid glue "clearly provides better adhesion" compared to PSA. While the tape failed the **Crush Test** immediately, the glued units survived.

### 3. Validation Results

Following the implementation of the gluing process, the Elvis units passed the full suite of "Critical" reliability gates that had previously failed:

- **Crush Test:** **PASSED**.
- **Drop Test:** **PASSED** (Glued units survived impact without separation).
- **High Temp & Humidity (HTHH):** **PASSED** (Surviving 65°C/90%RH environments).

### 4. The Cost of Reliability (Process Change)

To achieve this reliability, the manufacturing process required significant re-tooling:

- **Tooling Investment:** The switch to glue required the fabrication of a custom **"Clamping jig tool"** costing **$15,000** to hold the parts under pressure during curing.
- **Cycle Time:** The Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) was updated to require the units to remain in the clamping jig for **"at least 5 hours"** to ensure full structural integrity.

## DEEP DIVE: The "Cheep" Sound (Damping Grease Protocol)

Based on the forensic engineering logs and standard operating procedures (SOPs), the **Damping Grease / Lubrication Protocol** was a critical manufacturing intervention designed to resolve acoustic defects ("Cheep sound") and tactile quality issues ("Hollow feel").

### 1. The Trigger: Defect SAT-145 ("The Cheep")

During EVT2 Key Life Testing, the Elvis units exhibited a high-frequency noise described as a **"Cheep sound"** when pressing the main switch,.

- **Forensic Analysis:** Teardowns revealed that the **"spring contact surface has been scratched,"** indicating metal-on-plastic friction due to a lack of lubrication.
- **Tactile Failure:** Early units also suffered from poor tactile ratios (T.R. < 20%), contributing to a "bad" feeling switch.

### 2. The Protocol: Application & Verification

To permanently arrest this friction, I mandated a specific modification to the assembly SOP (Standard Operating Procedure):

- **The Material:** The specific Plan of Record (POR) identified in the SOP is **Dry Lubricant (A-1259F)**. Concurrently, the engineering team studied "damping grease" to work in tandem or as an enhancement to "absorb a loose fit" and provide a "quality feel",.
- **Application Point:** The SOP explicitly directs operators to **"Coating dry lubricant on the two side bottom carrier plane"**. This targets the sliding interface between the moving carrier and the static housing.
- **The UV Gate (Line Down Control):** Because the lubricant is transparent and difficult to verify with the naked eye, a **"UV lamp" inspection station** was integrated into the assembly line. The SOP instruction is: **"Use UV lamp to check dry lubricant"**,. This ensured 100% coverage and prevented un-lubricated units from escaping to the field.

### 3. The Engineering Logic (Rheology)

Unlike standard greases, **Damping Grease** is formulated with viscous synthetic oils that provide **high internal resistance to shear stress**.

- **The Effect:** This creates a "viscous drag" or controlled resistance, transforming the tactile feedback from a loose, plastic snap into a smooth, "velvet feel".
- **Acoustic Dampening:** The grease fills the microscopic voids between mating parts, eliminating the "Cheep" noise and the plastic "clack".

## DEEP DIVE: The "Yellowing" Gate (Delta E Control)

Based on the cosmetic specifications and forensic reliability logs, **Delta E ($\Delta E$) limits** ensured the visual consistency of the **Elvis** (Extension Switch) by converting subjective color perception into rigid pass/fail numerical data.

### 1. The Numerical "Kill" Limit ($\Delta E \le 1.5$)

While the general specification allowed for a color deviation of **$\Delta E < 3.0$**, the engineering team enforced a stricter standard for the painted/coated surfaces of the Elvis unit to ensure a premium look.

- **The Rule:** The **Cosmetic Specification (040-00020)** explicitly mandated that for **painted surfaces** (such as the UV-coated Elvis cap), the color difference must be **$\Delta E \le 1.5$** relative to the master standard.
- **The Measurement:** This was verified using a **Konica Minolta CM2600d Spectrophotometer**.
- **The Impact:** Any production unit deviating by more than 1.5 units from the "Golden Sample" was automatically rejected.

### 2. Environmental Stability (The "Yellowing" Gate)

Delta E limits were the primary forensic tool used to detect and reject the **UV coating failure** on the Elvis white plastic.

- **The Test:** Units were subjected to **UV Fade Testing** (ASTM G154) consisting of 12 cycles (96 hours total).
- **The Failure:** Reliability logs recorded that **"Elvis samples color changed after UV exposure test"**. The Spectrophotometer detected a shift beyond the **2.0 limit**, objectively proving the "White" finish was yellowing.
- **The Result:** This forced a re-evaluation of the **Betely UV hardcoat** and the **SABIC EXL9330** resin.

## DEEP DIVE: The "Rock Tumble" Benchmark (UV Deletion)

Based on the forensic engineering logs and reliability test plans, the **Rock Tumble Test** simulated the 10-year lifespan requirement through a **comparative benchmarking strategy** known as "The Time Machine" method.

### 1. The "Time Machine" Benchmark

Rather than calculating a theoretical wear rate, the engineering team calibrated the test using real-world data from competitors.

- **The Calibration:** They sourced competitor switches that had been active in the field for **1 year**.
- **The Simulation:** They placed new competitor units in the tumbler to determine exactly how many minutes of tumbling were required to replicate the wear patterns seen on the field units.
- **The Target:** The Sativa/Elvis units had to exhibit **less damage** than the competitors at these specific time intervals to verify they could survive the **10-year operational life**.

### 2. The "Aggressive Mix" Protocol

To accelerate a decade of abuse into an hour, the test utilized a **Thumler's Model B** rock tumbler filled with a specific abrasive cocktail:

- **The Media:** A mixture of **1 cup granite gravel**, **1 cup sand**, and **1 cup river pebbles**.
- **The Action:** This media created chaotic, multi-directional abrasion that attacked the surface finish.

### 3. Forensic Application: UV vs. Raw Resin

This simulation was the primary gatekeeper for the **"UV Deletion"** cost-reduction effort.

- **The Goal:** To prove that the raw **SABIC EXL9330** resin (uncoated) could match the scratch resistance of the competitor's coated products, allowing the team to remove the problematic UV coating that was causing "yellowing".
