# Project Elvis (Extension Switch) Forensic Report

## I. PROJECT SUMMARY

- **Role:** Head of Mechanical Engineering ("The Architect").
- **Mandate:** Engineer "Elvis" (Model N130), the wireless extension switch designed to replace standard 3-way switches and communicate with the Room Director.
- **Core Achievement:** Salvaged the EVT2 build from a **100% thermal-mechanical failure rate** (7/7 units) by diagnosing a critical CAD geometry mismatch and mandating a shift from PSA tape to liquid structural glue to secure the $79 SKU.

## II. THE ANATOMY OF FAILURE (Heuristic Analysis)

### Thermal/Mechanical Crisis: "The Floating Cap"

During EVT2 Thermal Stress testing (65°C to -20°C), the Elvis unit suffered a catastrophic structural failure designated as "Plastic Cap Floating."

- **The Event:** A **100% failure rate (7 of 7 units)** occurred where the user-facing cosmetic cap physically separated from the front housing.
- **The Forensic Find:** Analysis of **JIRA SAT-238** revealed a **0.1mm CAD geometry mismatch**. The internal pocket depth was **0.3mm** against a design intent of 0.2mm. The specified 0.25mm Nitto 57120W PSA tape was physically incapable of bridging the gap ("The Phantom Gap").
- **The Fallout:** The adhesive failed the **30kg Crush Test**, leading to immediate separation upon handling.

### Quality/Supply Chain: Acoustics & Audits

- **The "Cheep" Sound:** Users reported a "Cheep sound" (SAT-145) and hollow acoustics. The internal housing volume acted as a resonance chamber.
- **The 10-Year Mandate:** The requirement for a 10-year operational life was introduced retroactively, clashing with the initial "Consumer Electronics" grade architecture (2-year target).

## III. GOVERNANCE & RHYTHM

- **The Pulse:** The project followed a strict **Waterfall Strategy** with Functional Check Points.
- **Key Ruling:** I personally accepted the **"We Bend the Metal"** philosophy. When yield dropped to 50%, we did not stop the line; we engineered a manual rectification process ("The Blacksmithing Protocol") to keep production moving while tooling was corrected.
- **The Artifacts:**

## VI. DISCREET REPORTS

### Deep Dive: The "Floating Cap" (Adhesive Crisis)

Based on the forensic engineering logs, the transition to **liquid structural glue** was a "Stop Ship" intervention mandated to salvage the Elvis unit from a geometry-chemistry mismatch.

#### 1. The Root Cause: "The Phantom Gap"

Forensic metrology revealed a critical mismatch. The CAD design specified a **0.20mm** pocket, but actual molded parts measured **0.30mm**. The specified **Nitto 57120W PSA tape** (0.25mm) was physically floating in the 0.05mm void. The tape was not adhering; it was ghosting.

#### 2. The Intervention: Liquid Structural Bonding

I rejected the PSA tape and enforced a transition to **liquid glue**.

- **Gap Filling:** Liquid glue inherently filled the 0.1mm tolerance void, rendering the CAD mismatch irrelevant.
- **Bond Strength:** The glue formed a monolithic structural bond that passed **Drop** (1.3m) and **Crush** tests immediately.

#### 3. The Cost: $15,000 for Jigs

To achieve this, we invested **$15,000** in custom "Clamping Jigs" and extended the cycle time by **5 hours** to allow for curing under pressure. Reliability is not free; it is bought with time and tooling.

### Deep Dive: Structural Trauma (The "Crush" Logic)

The structural validation for the Elvis switch was not arbitrary; it was derived from anthropometric "Worst Case" modeling.

#### 1. The 30kg Crush Mandate

We reverse-engineered the "Crush" load using CDC data:

- **Baseline:** Average U.S. male body weight = **89 kg** (195.5 lbs).
- **The Physics:** We modeled an accidental trip/fall against the wall, assuming **1/3 of body mass** transfers to the device.
- **The Load:** **30 kg**.
- **The Test:** A 50mm diameter aluminum puck (simulating a shoulder) covered in 2mm foam (simulating tissue) was driven into the switch face. The PSA tape failed this test; the structural glue passed.

#### 2. The 100N Force Grid

To detect hidden injection molding weakness, we utilized a **100 Newton (10 kg)** probe test.

- **The Grid:** A rigid **3x3 matrix (9 points)** covering every zone of the faceplate.
- **The Weapon:** A 16mm steel rod.
- **Pass Criteria:** Zero permanent deformation at any coordinate. This mapped the enclosure's stiffness profile and validated the internal ribbing strategy.

### Deep Dive: Validating the 10-Year Life

The requirement for a **10-Year Operational Life** was introduced retroactively, forcing a "Time Machine" approach to reliability testing.

#### 1. The Usage Model (The Math of Aging)

The cycle counts were calculated deterministically:

- **Formula:** 10 Years × 365 Days × Daily Actuations × Safety Factor (1.5).
- **Main Switch:** 10 × 365 × 10 × 1.5 = **54,750 Cycles**. (Tested to **110,000**).
- **Air-Gap Switch:** Increased from standard to **100 cycles** to meet UL 1472 infrastructure grades.

#### 2. The "Rock Tumble" Benchmark

To visualize 10 years of wear on the plastic finish, we used a **Thumler's Model B** rock tumbler filled with a specific abrasive cocktail (Granite, Sand, River Pebbles).

- **The Benchmark:** We tumbled competitor switches that had seen **1 year** of field use to calibrate the wear pattern.
- **The Goal:** Prove that raw **SABIC EXL9330** resin (uncoated) could match the durability of coated competitors, allowing us to delete the UV coating that was causing "Yellowing" (Delta E failure).

### Deep Dive: The "Cheep" Sound (Acoustic Hygiene)

The "Standard Operating Procedure" (SOP) was rewritten to enforce acoustic quality.

#### 1. The Defect (SAT-145)

High-frequency squeaking ("Cheep") and a "hollow" feel were traced to **metal-on-plastic friction** and the resonance of the empty housing.

#### 2. The Intervention

- **Lubrication:** We mandated **Damping Grease** combined with **Dry Lubricant (A-1259F)** on the carrier planes.
- **Verification:** Integrated a **UV Lamp Station** on the assembly line to verify 100% lubricant coverage (the lubricant fluoresces under UV).
- **Ribbing:** Added internal structural ribs to break up the standing waves inside the housing, deadening the "hollow" signature.

### Forensic Audit: The JIRA Log

The **BAZ** (Bazooka/Base) and **SAT** (Sativa/Elvis) trackers reveal the specific forensic interventions executed by "The Architect" to prevent schedule collapse.

- **The "Wet Hands" Waiver:** When units failed the "Wet Hands" test due to minor oxidation, I intervened to prevent a Stop Ship. Ruling: _"For now, this test is still classified as 'information only'... OK to proceed with FAIL result."_ (Risk Management).
- **The shock Hazard (BAZ-22):** Identified a critical safety bug ("Electric shock when inserting switch"). Immediate priority resolution.
- **The "Yield Flip":** Identified a 50% yield loss on Fader Plates due to "Over-Constraint" (12 screws). Reduced to 6 screws, restoring yield to 100%.

**Conclusion:** The Elvis project was a lesson in **Rigidity as a Feature**. By enforcing strict manufacturing controls (Glue Jigs, UV Gates, 100N Grids), we converted a fragile 0% yield prototype into a 10-year infrastructure product.
