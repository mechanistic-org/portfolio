## The Challenge

> **Context:** The Makeline was not just a machine; it was a factory in a box. The core challenge was **System Integration**.

While individual modules (Dispensers) solved the physics of moving ingredients, the Makeline had to orchestrate them. We had to coordinate 350+ actuators, managing power distribution, thermal loads, and CAN bus traffic, all within a violently wash-down sanitary environment.
The failure mode wasn't "It broke"—it was "Latency drift caused the guacamole to miss the bowl by 10ms."

## Engineering Approach

My role shifted from "Component Designer" to "System Architect." I treated the mechanical assembly as a physical network topology.

- **Modular Architecture:** We decoupled the "Payload Modules" (Dispensers) from the "Chassis" (Power/Data/Motion). This allowed parallel development: The chassis team could optimize frame rigidity and cable routing while the dispenser team iterated on portion accuracy.
- **The "Nervous System":** We developed a unified cabling harness that was both modular and IP69K waterproof. This wasn't just "wiring"; it was designing a physical API that enforced correct assembly.
- **Integration Testing:** I built the "Iron Bird" rig—a skeleton chassis that allowed us to test full-system electrical and firmware loads before the actual stainless steel frame had even returned from the fab.

## Impact

The system achieved a throughput of **350 meals per hour**, running autonomously for 4-hour peak shifts.

- **Reliability:** The modular approach reduced Mean Time To Repair (MTTR) from hours to minutes (hot-swappable modules).
- **Scalability:** The architecture became the foundation for Hyphen's entire product line, scaling from "Burrito Lines" to "Salad Stations" without redesigning the core chassis.
