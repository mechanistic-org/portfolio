# WebTV Cortez

## Project summary

- **Role boundary:** Mechanical-design and surface-development consultant responsible for industrial-design liaison, mechanical layout, Pro/ENGINEER surfacing, and prototype support. Product requirements, industrial design, electrical design, vendor tooling, and production decisions remained cross-functional responsibilities.
- **Mandate:** Translate the organic Cortez wireless-keyboard concept into a hard-model package supporting a full-size QWERTY layout, dedicated WebTV buttons, navigation controls, integrated remote functions, and handheld or tabletop use.
- **Evidence-bounded result:** After multiple design reviews and surface revisions, the final CAD was released to prototype in six weeks. The surviving project statement describes the machined surfaces as flawless; that qualitative result is attributed rather than presented as independent inspection evidence.

## Surface development

The design combined a conventional key field with highly organic left and right grip regions, a sculpted back, and co-molded Santoprene grip intent. The local project record says the rear surface transitions were more difficult than the front and that the Pro/ENGINEER surfaces were intended to drive a hard model for use as a reference by overseas production facilities.

Early-ID sections, grip views, and the functional-model packet preserve the shape-development surface. They support the form and prototype story, but do not establish the former public claims about a 3 to 15 degree IR correction, a 43-hour task breakdown, an exact hard-model review outcome, or a named technology-showcase deadline. Those details have been removed because the current primary Cortez packet does not resolve them.

## Key layout and tooling estimate

Silitek's May 18, 2000 arrangement identifies 19.05 mm pitch and standard low-profile keys. It estimates $30,000 to tool two keys whose widths fell outside the standard set. That is a vendor estimate, not a recorded expenditure.

The bounded archive does not establish the former 19.00 mm industrial-design baseline, an accumulated 0.05 mm stop-ship collision, removal of a rightmost key column, or avoidance of the tooling estimate. The current candidate therefore reports the documented layout and estimate without inventing the design decision that followed.

## Schedule and billing

The local project statement says the final CAD was released to prototype in six weeks. Three invoices independently bound a concentrated June work period:

- June 8: 100 hours and $10,000.
- June 20: 98 hours and $9,800.
- June 27: 51 hours and $5,100.

Together they document 249 hours and $24,900 of Cortez keyboard surface development. They do not support the former 43-hour task decomposition.
