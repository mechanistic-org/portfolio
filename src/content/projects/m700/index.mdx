## The Challenge

> **Context:** Legal requirements meant users had to have the physical disc present to play the digital copy.

The M700 Disc Vault was a **Optical Jukebox** that held 320 discs.

- **Robotics:** It needed to automatically ingest, identify, and catalog discs dropped into the slot by a user.
- **Reliability:** The mechanism had to handle thousands of load/unload cycles without scratching a disc or jamming.

## Engineering Approach

I worked on the "Picker" mechanism—the robotic hand that grabbed the disc.

- **Compliance:** The gripper had built-in mechanical compliance to tolerate misaligned discs (up to 2mm) without jamming.
- **Sensor Saturation:** We used IR break-beams to detect disc presence, but the highly reflective surface of Blu-rays caused false positives. I implemented a polarized filter system to kill the glare.

### Forensic Engineering

> **The "Vanishing" Disc (Mechanism Failure):**
> Discs inserted backwards were invisible to the optical sensors, causing the robot to "double load" and jam.
>
> - **The Fix:** Redesigned the pinch roller assembly with **double-strength springs** and **solid mounts** (replacing rubber grommets) to prevent shaft deflection under higher load.
> - **The "Butter Knife" Protocol:** Field reports indicated EMI gaskets could deform into the slot path. Dealers had to use a specific technique with a shim (or butter knife) to clear the obstruction.
