# D-Control (Buckley)

## Project summary

- **Role boundary:** Lead Mechanical Engineer for the stand sheet metal, structural plastics, assembly methods, and PCB dimensional-control drawings. Electrical design, software, sourcing, factory execution, and the adjacent D-Command program remained cross-functional responsibilities.
- **Mandate:** Turn Buckley into a modular steel control surface that could scale from a main unit plus fader units to a five-module, 80-fader installation while keeping the stand and cosmetic interfaces buildable.
- **Core result:** More than half of the first 42 fader pans were rejected after PEM insertion bowed the steel. The recovery combined immediate cold work with a supplier process change, while an estimated two-month manufacturing campaign was executed in 2.5 weeks. A separate prototype-board investigation contained a 90% V-2 lot before shipment.

## The anatomy of failure

### Fader-pan crisis: more than half rejected

PEM hardware insertion stretched the fader-pan sheet steel point by point. The first 42-part batch arrived bowed far enough that more than half were rejected and the side-plate interfaces would not align.

The response separated containment from correction. On the floor, the pans were cold-worked over a table edge to recover flatness. At the supplier, PEM insertion was redistributed and a flattening operation was added after insertion. The surviving ruling supports both actions, but not the former claim that the pilot line never stopped or that every field failure was eliminated.

### Stand fit: holes, bosses, and side plates

The stand plastics followed a specific D-Control change trail. ECO 6310 enlarged mounting holes from 0.200 inch to 0.250 inch, enlarged the surrounding bosses, and raised five bosses by 0.050 inch. Deviation 1212 captured the interim ream operation; the Rev B part record specifies Noryl. ECO 8197 belongs to the side-plate relief work. PPI appears only as the named molder, so no broader design ownership is assigned to it.

These facts resolve a long-running attribution error. ECO 8114, Vegas Mode, sticky buttons, and the plastic Faraday-cage story belong to D-Command. They are not part of the D-Control stand narrative.

### Five-module tolerance and assembly

The configuration record models a five-module installation spanning roughly ten feet. It uses a 23.079-inch unit width plus 0.020-inch tolerance and carries C-Mid and D-Mid rail calculations to four decimal places. That precision describes the design record; it is not presented as a measured end-to-end installation tolerance.

The public D-Control guide independently documents the ratcheting-strap operation used while joining main and fader units. The strap is therefore treated as an explicit assembly method, not a colorful inference about how installers forced the structure together.

### Prototype PCB crisis: V-2 containment

One unnamed vendor supplied a prototype-board population in which 90% carried V-2 material. Four different shop logos indicated uncontrolled subcontracting. The response was containment and supplier governance: affected prototypes were quarantined, customer exposure was checked, and the vendor was terminated for prototype work. The correction record states that zero affected boards shipped.

### The 2.5-week campaign

The June 2004 correction record describes a manufacturing and DFM campaign estimated at two months and executed in 2.5 weeks. Home-built jigs shortened the path to usable assembly tooling. This is reported as a schedule compression in the surviving record, without converting it into an unsupported revenue number.

## Governance and evidence

The public boundary is now explicit: Buckley is D-Control and its structural record is steel; Danko is D-Command and owns the plastic Faraday-cage and ECO 8114 history. The $50-$100 XMON cost delta is shared Buckley/Danko context, so it is not counted as D-Control-only impact.

NotebookLM was used as a historical research surface, not as publication authority. All 35 sources, 29 notes, 16 audio artifacts, and the semantic content of 29 mind maps are locally accounted for in a dated reconciliation. NotebookLM rejected visual-layout exports for the mind maps, so their layouts are not claimed as preserved. The former public audio briefing is no longer linked because it repeats cross-project attribution drift.

## Quantified impact

- **Rejected** more than half of the first 42 bowed fader pans.
- **Executed** an estimated two-month manufacturing campaign in 2.5 weeks.
- **Defined** a five-module stand record with 23.079-inch unit width, 0.020-inch tolerance, and C-Mid/D-Mid rail calculations carried to 0.0001 inch.
- **Contained** a prototype population that was 90% V-2 with zero affected boards shipped.
- **Owned** a 26-release stand sheet-metal record plus the structural plastics and PCB dimensional-control surfaces under a solo mechanical mandate.
