# PROJECT BUCKLEY: FORENSIC ANALYSIS

## The Crisis: Adhesion Failure

### The Symptom

During Pilot Validation for the PCII (D-Control) Fader Bank, the cosmetic overlay began delaminating from the sub-chassis. This was not a minor cosmetic defect; the peeling overlay physically blocked the path of the motorized faders, rendering the console unusable.

### The Impact

- **Functional Failure:** Motorized faders jammed against the peeling graphic.
- **Brand Failure:** For a $30,000 flagship console, "peeling stickers" were an unacceptable build quality failure.
- **Schedule Failure:** A "Stop Ship" order was imminent, threatening the launch window.

### The Initial Theory

The vendor immediately blamed the adhesive ("Bad Glue") and proposed switching vendors. I rejected this scattershot approach as "Guesswork" and paused the line to conduct a proper investigation.

---

## The Investigation: Forensic DOE

### Surface Energy Analysis

I suspected the issue wasn't the glue, but the surface it was bonding to. I designed a Design of Experiments (DOE) to isolate the root cause, testing three variables:

1.  **Adhesive:** 3M 467MP vs. 3M 200MP.
2.  **Surface Prep:** Standard IPA Wipe vs. Plasma Treatment.
3.  **Substrate:** ABS vs. PC/ABS blend.

### The Smoking Gun

Detailed forensic analysis using Dyne Pens revealed that the molded chassis had critically low **Surface Energy**. The plastic was effectively "non-stick" (like Teflon) due to mold release agents and the material's inherent properties. The adhesive wasn't failing; it was being repelled at the molecular level.

## The Resolution: Plasma Treatment

We implemented a **Plasma Treatment** station line-side at the vendor. This process bombarded the plastic surface with ionized gas, temporarily breaking the molecular bonds and raising the surface energy to >50 dynes/cm.

**Result:** The overlay bond became permanent. The "peeling" defect was eliminated, and the D-Control shipped on time with zero field failures related to adhesion.
